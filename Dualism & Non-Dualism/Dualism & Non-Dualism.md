# Dualism & Non-Dualism

## Question

We can observe that people who experience accident and injury in the head can change their behavior by that accident. For instance, someone who has hit in the left brain and survive, their behavior has been change and its not like them before. Consider Phineas Gage, a railroad worker whose personality changed after a severe frontal lobe injury. Does it mean that the duality (physical and non physical) is false as we are just biological and natural processes which is physical?

---

If we accept that all aspects of our behavior and personality arise from physical processes in the brain, this view aligns with **physicalism**—the idea that everything about the mind can be explained by physical processes and natural laws. Let us critically evaluate each premise of this perspective alongside the counter-arguments offered by dualism and non-dualism.

### **1. Dualism and the Brain**

Dualism, as traditionally proposed by philosophers like René Descartes (substance dualism), argues that the mind (or soul) is a non-physical entity distinct from the brain.

* **Physicalist Premise**: Cases like Phineas Gage show that damage to specific brain regions drastically changes personality, executive function, and morality. Additionally, brain-imaging studies show direct neural correlates for thoughts, emotions, and choices. If a non-physical mind operated independently, physical brain destruction should not alter the mind's internal nature or character.
* **Dualist Counter-Challenge**: Dualists argue that the physicalist conclusion misinterprets the *instrumental* relationship between the mind and the brain.
* **The Receiver/Instrument Analogy**: Dualists compare the brain to a radio receiver and the mind to the transmitted signal. If you damage a radio's circuitry, the sound comes out distorted, static-filled, or silent. This distortion does not prove the radio broadcast itself was altered; it proves the *medium of expression* was corrupted.
* Under substance dualism, brain damage impairs the physical vehicle through which the non-physical self interacts with and expresses itself in the physical world, not necessarily the non-physical self itself.



### **2. Biological and Natural Processes**

The view that humans are entirely biological organisms governed by physical laws suggests a **monist** framework:

* **Physicalist Premise**: Behavior, decision-making, and emotional states trace back to neural architecture, neurochemistry, and electrical impulses. All observable human mental phenomena can be altered via physical interventions (trauma, neurosurgery, psychotropic medication) without requiring a non-physical explanation.
* **Dualist & Non-Dualist Counter-Challenge**:
* **Correlation vs. Causation**: Showing that neural activity *correlates* with mental states does not prove that physical processes *generate* mental states.
* **Bidirectional Neuroplasticity**: The mind can actively reshape the physical brain. Mentally directed focus, cognitive behavioral therapy, and meditation physically alter neural pathways (neuroplasticity). If the mind were purely a passive byproduct of brain chemistry, top-down conscious intention could not alter physical brain structures.



### **3. Does This Disprove Dualism?**

While physicalism offers a streamlined model for cognitive science, severe brain injuries do not outright disprove dualism:

* **Physicalist Argument (Occam’s Razor)**: Physicalism posits that physical cause leads to physical effect. Introducing a non-physical soul creates the "Interaction Problem"—how an immaterial substance acts upon physical matter without violating laws of conservation of energy.
* **Dualist Argument (The Hard Problem of Consciousness)**: Physicalism struggles to explain *qualia*—the subjective, first-person "what it feels like" experience of seeing red or feeling pain. Explaining brain circuits (the "easy problems") does not explain why or how conscious awareness emerges from non-conscious matter.
* **Non-Dualist Alternative**: Non-dualism (such as Advaita Vedanta or Idealism) rejects both physicalism and Cartesian dualism. It argues that physical matter and individual brains are appearances *within* consciousness, rather than consciousness being produced *by* the brain. Damage to a brain alters the local expression of awareness, much like a smudged lens distorts light without altering the nature of the light source.

### **4. Implications of Physicalism**

* **Free Will & Moral Responsibility**: If human action stems strictly from physical antecedent causes (genetics, neural wiring, environmental triggers), free will may be an illusion.
* **Malleable Identity**: If identity is strictly a byproduct of physical architecture, any alteration to brain tissue permanently reshapes the person, challenging traditional notions of an enduring "self."

---

## **Conclusion**

Cases like Phineas Gage demonstrate that **our personality, behavior, and cognitive faculties depend heavily on physical brain integrity**. However, this empirical evidence **does not conclusively disprove dualism or non-dualism**:

1. **Physicalism** remains the dominant framework in neuroscience because it avoids introducing non-physical entities (applying Occam's Razor).
2. **Dualism** survives this challenge by framing the brain as the necessary *physical interface* or transmitter for a non-physical mind. Brain damage distorts the output of the mind without disproving its immaterial existence.
3. **Non-Dualism** reframes the debate altogether, proposing that consciousness is the fundamental foundation of reality, and the physical brain is merely the localized channel through which individualized experience occurs.

While physical brain injury proves that our *expressed psychology* is biological, whether the core of *subjective awareness itself* is purely physical remains an open philosophical question.

**Therefore,** this is still undecidable.
