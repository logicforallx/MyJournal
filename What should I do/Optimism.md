# Optimism

## Is optimism good?

## Definition

* Good: Rightly or validly desirable, favorable, acceptable or positive.
* Optimism: A thoughts, belief or hope that the outcome of some specific endeavor or outcomes in general will be good.

## Logical Reasoning

1. Optimism increases the likelihood of effort:
    * An optimistic person believes that success is possible or likely, which motivates them to put in the necessary effort. [Observation]
    * Study: A study by Carver et al. (2010) on the relationship between optimism and coping found that optimistic individuals are more likely to engage in proactive coping and effortful strategies. They are more likely to take actions to achieve their goals because they believe in a positive outcome.
    * Source: Carver, C. S., Scheier, M. F., & Segerstrom, S. C. (2010). Optimism. Clinical Psychology Review, 30(7), 879-889.
2. Pessimism decreases the likelihood of effort:
    * A pessimistic person believes that success is unlikely or not worth pursuing, which decreases their motivation to put in effort. [Observation]
    * Study: Research by Norem and Cantor (1986) demonstrated that pessimistic individuals are less likely to engage in effortful behavior because they anticipate failure. This belief decreases their motivation to try and achieve goals.
    * Source: Norem, J. K., & Cantor, N. (1986). Defensive pessimism: Keep the anxiety in its place. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 51(6), 1208-1217.
3. Effort increases the probability of success of goodness:
    * While effort does not guarantee success, it increases the chances of achieving a positive outcome compared to not putting in effort at all. [Observation]
    * Study: Studies have shown that effortful engagement and persistence are strong predictors of success. For instance, Duckworth et al. (2007) found that grit, which involves sustained effort and interest in long-term goals, predicts success in various domains.
    * Source: Duckworth, A. L., Peterson, C., Matthews, M. D., & Kelly, D. R. (2007). Grit: Perseverance and passion for long-term goals. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 92(6), 1087-1101.
4. Not putting in effort reduces the probability of success of goodness: 
    * Without effort, the likelihood of achieving success is significantly reduced or eliminated. [Observation]
    * Study: A meta-analysis by Mullen and Monin (2006) highlights that lack of effort significantly impacts achievement outcomes, as effort is a key component in reaching goals and achieving success.
    * Source: Mullen, B., & Monin, B. (2006). Self-presentation and the role of effort in success: A meta-analysis. Personality and Social Psychology Review, 10(2), 147-161.
5. Optimism, by encouraging effort, increases the potential for success of goodness while the otherwise decrease it. [1,3,2,4]
6. Is optimism more reasonable and good than pessimism in the sense that it maximizes the potential for success of goodness? [6]

## Scientific Evidence: 

Here is additional scientific evidence showing that optimism is more reasonable and beneficial than pessimism, particularly in maximizing the potential for success and well-being:

1. **Optimism and Achievement:**
   - **Study:** Research by Roberts et al. (2007) shows that optimism is associated with better academic and career outcomes. Optimistic individuals are more likely to set and achieve goals due to their positive expectations and persistence.
   - **Source:** Roberts, B. W., & Smith, J. L. (2007). Personality and performance in the workplace: The influence of personality traits on performance. *International Journal of Selection and Assessment, 15*(2), 168-180.

2. **Optimism and Health Outcomes:**
   - **Study:** A meta-analysis by Rasmussen et al. (2009) found that optimism is linked to better physical health and longevity. Optimistic individuals have lower levels of stress and inflammation, leading to better health outcomes.
   - **Source:** Rasmussen, H. N., Scheier, M. F., & Greenhouse, J. B. (2009). Optimism and physical health: A meta-analytic review. *Health Psychology, 28*(3), 275-287.

3. **Optimism and Stress Management:**
   - **Study:** Research by Carver and Scheier (2014) indicates that optimism helps individuals manage stress more effectively. Optimists are better at using adaptive coping strategies, which helps them handle challenges and stress more successfully.
   - **Source:** Carver, C. S., & Scheier, M. F. (2014). *Optimism, pessimism, and self-regulation*. In R. A. Bailey & S. E. Coontz (Eds.), *Handbook of Self-Regulation* (pp. 367-385). Routledge.

4. **Optimism and Financial Success:**
   - **Study:** A longitudinal study by Fredrickson et al. (2008) found that optimism is associated with better financial outcomes. Optimistic individuals tend to have better financial stability and make more effective financial decisions.
   - **Source:** Fredrickson, B. L., Cohn, M. A., Coffey, K., Pek, J., & Finkel, S. E. (2008). Open hearts build lives: Positive emotions, induced through loving-kindness meditation, build consequential personal resources. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 95*(5), 1045-1062.

5. **Optimism and Social Relationships:**
   - **Study:** Research by Lyubomirsky et al. (2005) shows that optimism enhances social relationships. Optimistic individuals are more likely to form and maintain positive social connections, which contribute to their overall well-being and success.
   - **Source:** Lyubomirsky, S., King, L., & Diener, E. (2005). The benefits of frequent positive affect: Does happiness lead to success? *Psychological Bulletin, 131*(6), 803-855.

6. **Optimism and Work Performance:**
   - **Study:** A study by Judge and Bono (2001) demonstrates that optimism is positively related to job performance. Optimistic employees are more likely to achieve higher performance levels due to their proactive attitude and resilience.
   - **Source:** Judge, T. A., & Bono, J. E. (2001). Relationship of core self-evaluations traits—self-esteem, generalised self-efficacy, locus of control, and emotional stability—with job satisfaction and job performance: A meta-analysis. *Journal of Applied Psychology, 86*(1), 80-92.

These studies collectively reinforce the idea that optimism is more beneficial than pessimism. Optimism enhances various aspects of life, including achievement, health, stress management, financial success, social relationships, and work performance, thereby maximizing the potential for success and well-being.
