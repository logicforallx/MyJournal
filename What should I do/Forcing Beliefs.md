# Is Forcing Beliefs Good?

1. Belief requires internal assent: *Belief means judgement, stand, thought, or subjective acceptance that something is the case, a statement is true, that something exists, and the likes. That requires personal acceptance (internal assent).*

2. Force cannot cause internal assent: *Force means make (someone) do something against their will (internal assent or acceptance). Something that is against (contradictory) their will (internal assent or acceptance) is not internal assent. So force cannot cause internal assent*

3. Therefore force cannot cause belief: *This is base from 1 and 2.*

4. If something cannot cause belief, then it is irrational (not in accordance) for genuine belief formation: *This is base from it's implication.*

5. Therefore forcing belief is irrational (not in accordance) for genuine belief formation: *This is base from 3 and 4.*

## Additional Analytical Corollaries

### Corollary 1: Epistemic Failure

If a belief is true, it should withstand rational scrutiny. Forcing it implies lack of confidence in its rational persuasiveness.

---

### Corollary 2: Self-Contradiction

If one believes a claim is true, the rational approach is to present reasons. Forcing belief contradicts the premise that truth persuades by evidence.

---

### Corollary 3: Information Loss

Coercion suppresses disagreement, preventing error correction and reducing overall knowledge accuracy.
