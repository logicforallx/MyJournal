## Other Thinkers Recommendation

Several major thinkers across philosophy, psychology, and theology have arrived at this conclusion: that purely rational analysis of an uncertain world leads to existential paralysis or nihilism, and that a deliberate commitment (a leap, posture, or trust) to ultimate goodness is the necessary engine for meaning, action, and subjective realization

* **Søren Kierkegaard (Existential Philosophy):** Explicitly argued that objective certainty is impossible in existential matters; thus, the "leap of faith" is the only mechanism to transcend despair and achieve authentic subjective realization and alignment with the Divine (Maximal Goodness).
* **Leo Tolstoy (Christian Anarchism & Literature):** In his later philosophical works (e.g., *A Confession*, *The Kingdom of God Is Within You*), he concluded that rational intellect alone leads to existential nihilism, and that a conscious, active faith in universal love/goodness is the necessary foundation for true life, meaning, and happiness.
* **William James (Pragmatism & Philosophy of Mind):** In *The Will to Believe*, he argued that when proof is unavailable, choosing to believe in ultimate goodness is rationally justified, as the belief itself generates the subjective truth and unlocks positive real-world outcomes.
* **Immanuel Kant (Moral Philosophy):** In *Critique of Practical Reason*, he concluded that while pure intellect cannot empirically prove absolute goodness, practical reason must postulate it *as if* it exists to make moral action coherent and prevent existential despair.
* **Blaise Pascal (Mathematics & Existential Theology):** In *Pensées*, he argued that finite reason has limits, and taking a voluntary stance of trust toward ultimate goodness allows the mind to experience the peace, order, and meaning that logic alone cannot deliver.
* **Viktor Frankl (Logotherapy & Existential Psychology):** In *Man’s Search for Meaning*, he demonstrated that holding a baseline faith in an ultimate, transcendent meaning (*Super-Meaning*) is the vital psychological posture that preserves internal agency, resilience, and subjective good.
* **Mahatma Gandhi (Practical Ethics):** Influenced by Tolstoy, he maintained that while absolute Truth cannot be fully comprehended by limited human intellect, acting with unshakeable faith in universal goodness enables us to perceive and realize it in practice.
