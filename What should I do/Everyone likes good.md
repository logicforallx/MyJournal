## Socrates View

Socrates built this philosophical stance on two core proofs:

**The Universal Desire for the Good:** Socrates famously argued in Plato's Gorgias and Meno that all people, whether they are a wise person or a tyrant, desire the good. When someone does something bad (like a thief stealing or a dictator executing a rival), they do not desire the bad thing itself. Instead, they desire the perceived benefit of that action, such as wealth, power, or security, which they mistakenly believe will make them happy.

**No One Does Wrong Willingly:** Socrates asserted that "no one does wrong willingly or knowingly". He argued that a rational person would never intentionally choose self-harm or misery. Therefore, any wrong act is purely the result of ignorance—a failure of the intellect to correctly calculate what is truly good and beneficial in the long run.

## Socratic Analogy: The Optical Illusion of Time

To understand the logic naturally, Socrates compared this to visual perspective:

1. An object placed right next to your eye looks massive.
2. A mountain miles away looks tiny.
3. If you do not know the math of distance, you will argue the close object is physically larger than the mountain.

Socrates argued that human emotions do the exact same thing to time. Immediate pleasure is right next to our eye, so it looks "massively good." Future consequences are far away, so they look "microscopically small." The deduction proves that vice is not a failure of character or a wicked will; it is a failure of mental measurement. 


## Scrutiny

* The Modern Contrast:This Socratic view is largely challenged by modern philosophy and psychology. While Socrates believed the intellect determines the will (if you know what is good, you will naturally do it), modern thought often acknowledges the existence of akrasia (weakness of will). Today, other philosophers accept that humans can clearly see and understand what is good, yet still choose to act against that knowledge due to overpowering emotions, addictions, or immediate desires .
    - What if overpowering emotions, addictions, and short-term desires do not disprove his theory, instead, they actually prove it? When a person gives in to an addiction or an emotional outburst, they are not choosing evil because it is evil. They are choosing that action because, in that exact moment, their judgment is clouded, and they perceive the immediate pleasure or relief as a "greater good" than the long-term consequence, does it?
    - Regard socrates view also:
        - The Illusion of the Present Moment: Socrates argued that immediate desires distort our internal "scale" of measurement. Just as an object looks larger when it is close to your eyes and smaller when it is far away, an immediate pleasure (like taking a drug or venting anger) looks massive and good right now. The long-term consequence (like health issues or broken relationships) is far away, so it looks tiny and insignificant. The person hasn't chosen to harm themselves; they have succumbed to an illusion of perspective.
        - For Socrates, ignorance does not just mean being uneducated. It means a temporary failure of knowledge. When strong emotions or addictions take over, a person experiences a form of temporary madness or blindness. The knowledge of what is truly good is not actively functioning in their mind; it has been suppressed by the immediate promise of relief.

* How about the behavior of self sabotage?
    - Are they wanting or choosing it for the sake of badness or unfavorable? Or do they just perceive it as favorable?
    - They are not choosing to ruin their life because they love ruin. They are choosing immediate protection from emotional pain. Avoidance is what is "in accordance with their being" at that micro-second, so they perceive it as favorable. Does it?

* How about psychopath?
    - Are they wanting or choosing it for the sake of badness or unfavorable? Or do they just perceive it as favorable?
    - They are still seeking what they perceive as good (favorable to them). They simply have a radically distorted, ego-centric definition of what is "in accordance" with their existence. They are blind to the universal social good because their brain chemistry does not perceive it as desirable. Does it?
