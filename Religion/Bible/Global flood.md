# Global Flood - Issues and Defence

## --- 

A regional flood is implausible because of the principle that:

> **“Water seeks its own level.”**

According to the biblical narrative, after seven months:

*“the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.”*

The mountain has an elevation of **5,137 meters**.

The Philippines would still be submerged at that level.

**Mount Apo = 2,954 m**

And almost the entire world would still be underwater, with only mountains higher than Ararat remaining above the water. Those areas would also be uninhabitable for many land animals because of the icy conditions.

So the regional-flood explanation still seems implausible, especially considering that there are **millions of species of land animals**.


## Below is the possible response for this issue:


If you're referring to **Mount Ararat** in modern-day Turkey, it stands at about **5,137 meters (16,854 feet)** high. That’s correct.

But now let’s get logically sharp about this. When **Genesis 8:4** says:

> "*the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat*"

It uses the plural: "*mountains*" (*hare Ararat* in Hebrew), not necessarily pointing to that specific peak (the modern Mount Ararat). Here are the logical facts:

* **The "Mountains of Ararat"** refer broadly to the ancient kingdom or region of **Urartu** — which included a mountainous area in eastern Turkey, Armenia, and Iran. So, it doesn’t specify *the highest peak*.

* **Mount Ararat (Agri Dağı)**, the massive volcanic peak that stands at 5,137 meters, became *traditionally* associated with the ark's resting place later in Christian and Islamic traditions — but the Bible itself doesn’t say "*on Mount Ararat,*" only "*in the mountains of Ararat.*"

---

### Logical deduction:

* If the ark rested on the current Mount Ararat's summit (5,137 m), this would imply that floodwaters rose to cover even the tallest peaks in that region — aligning with a *global flood* reading.

* But if "*mountains of Ararat*" refers more loosely to the surrounding lower mountains, then it could suggest a *regional flood* scenario, where not every 5,000+ meter peak was submerged.

Thus, the height of 5,137 meters is correct for **modern Mount Ararat**, but whether that was the exact mountain meant by Genesis is historically and geographically debatable.

---

---

### ✅ **Arguments *for* the floodwaters reaching over 5,000 meters (Global Flood View)**

This view says that the Genesis flood was worldwide, covering even the highest mountains like modern Mount Ararat (5,137 m).

1. **Textual Basis**
   Genesis 7:19 says:

   > "*And the waters prevailed so mightily on the earth that all the high mountains under the whole heaven were covered.*"
   > This sounds universal and total — not local. If "*all*" the high mountains were covered, then Ararat (at 5,137 m) and even taller peaks (like Everest at 8,848 m) would have been submerged.

2. **Ark’s resting place**
   If the ark rested *on* Ararat, then logically the flood must have at least reached that elevation.

3. **Theological Motivation**
   Proponents argue that the flood was God’s judgment on all humankind (Genesis 6:13), implying a global scale. A local flood would fail to explain why Noah had to build an ark to save "every kind of animal" when they could have just migrated.

---

### ❌ **Arguments *against* the floodwaters reaching 5,000+ meters (Problems with a Global Flood Reaching Ararat’s Peak)**

1. **Physical Impossibility (Water Volume)**
   To cover **Mount Everest** (8,848 m) or even **Mount Ararat** (5,137 m), earth would need **3–4 times** its current ocean water. There simply isn't enough water on Earth today — all existing water (oceans, ice caps, rivers, ground) falls far short.
   *Conclusion*: Either supernatural addition/removal of water occurred, or the story isn't meant to describe a global physical event.

2. **Atmospheric Pressure and Oxygen**
   If waters rose above 5,000 m:

   * Air pressure at that altitude is **about half** that at sea level.
   * Oxygen would be thin; humans and animals not adapted would struggle to survive even in an ark.

3. **Mountains May Have Been Lower Then?**
   Some young-earth creationists claim that mountains like Ararat and Everest rose after the flood (due to tectonic upheaval).
   *Problem*: This is speculative and lacks solid geological support. Modern geology shows that many mountain ranges predate any proposed flood date.

4. **Textual Alternative: "Mountains" Means Hills or Regional Peaks**
   The Hebrew word "*har*" translated as "*mountain*" can also mean "*hill*." So the "*mountains of Ararat*" could refer to smaller ranges in the region, not 5,000 m peaks.
   *So*: This opens the door for a **regional flood** interpretation — still devastating but not literally covering every mountain on Earth.

---

### 🔎 **Neutral Deduction**

* If you **insist** the ark rested on modern Mount Ararat (5,137 m), then you are logically *committed* to a flood at least that deep — meaning a **global flood model**.
* But this leads to **physical problems** (insufficient water volume, pressure issues, geological inconsistency).

Thus:

* A **global flood** reaching above 5,000 m requires *miraculous/supernatural intervention* (divine addition and removal of water).
* A **regional flood** interpretation avoids these physical problems and aligns with archaeology, but then you'd have to argue that "*all the high mountains*" is Near Eastern hyperbole rather than literal global fact.

---

### 🟦 **Conclusion**

* A fulfilled prophecy about the ark resting on *the mountains of Ararat* does not necessarily prove a 5,000 m global flood unless you also accept supernatural assumptions.
* If you reject those supernatural additions, then the text must be reinterpreted regionally (lower elevations, less than 5,000 m).
* Therefore, claiming "*the ark landed at 5,137 m, so the flood was global*" **only works** if you're also ready to argue for supernatural intervention overriding natural law.
