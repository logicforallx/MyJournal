# Speciesism

Speciesism is define as the assumption of human superiority leading to the exploitation of animals. This might include not respecting other's life like animals, etc. It seems that the bible morality is speciesism but is it morally right?

---


### 🔥 **Biblical Facts About Animal Killing and Sacrifice**

1. **Animals are killed for food**

   * **Genesis 9:3** — "*Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you; and as I gave you the green plants, I give you everything.*"

     * After the Flood, God explicitly allows Noah and his descendants to eat animals.
   * **Leviticus 11** — Gives rules about which animals are clean and can be eaten.
   * **New Testament (Acts 10:13-15)** — Peter is told "*Kill and eat,*" abolishing old dietary restrictions.

2. **Animal sacrifice is commanded**

   * **Leviticus 1–7** — Entire sections detailing how to kill animals (burnt offerings, sin offerings, guilt offerings) to please God.
   * **Exodus 29:18** — "*It is a burnt offering to the Lord... an aroma pleasing to the Lord.*"
   * God is described as enjoying the "*pleasing aroma*" of burning flesh (which, logically, is a human way of saying He accepts the act).

3. **No command to abolish animal killing**

   * Nowhere in the Old or New Testament does God command, "*Stop killing animals; it is immoral.*"
   * In fact, even in the end-times prophecy (**Ezekiel 40-46**), **animal sacrifices are depicted as continuing** in a restored temple.

---

### 🔥 **Deductions from These Facts**

1. ✅ **Bible God allows and even commands the killing of sentient animals**

   * This is directly stated multiple times.

2. ✅ **Bible God receives animal death as offerings**

   * He considers them *pleasing* or *acceptable*.
   * In OT Judaism, animal sacrifice was central worship.

3. ✅ **Bible God does not command humans to stop killing animals**

   * No verse says, "*All killing of animals is wrong, stop this practice.*"
   * Jesus and apostles participated in eating meat (Passover lamb, fish meals).

---

### 💥 **Now the Moral Question: Is This Right?**

* **Modern moral principle**: Sentient beings capable of suffering deserve moral consideration. Killing them without necessity is wrong.
  (This is the logic used by animal rights thinkers like Peter Singer.)

* **Biblical position**:

  * Animal death is accepted, even used as worship.
  * No hint that animal suffering is a moral evil in itself.
  * Only human sin is central; animal suffering is peripheral or ritual.

---

### 🔎 **Possible Apologist Defenses (with Logical Testing)**

1. **"Animals aren't morally equal to humans."**

   * ✔️ This is consistent with biblical theology.
   * ❌ But it fails if you accept that suffering matters morally regardless of species (sentience-based ethics).

2. **"Animal death is necessary because of the Fall — before that, animals were not killed."**

   * ✔️ Genesis 1:29–30 suggests early humans were vegetarian.
   * ❌ But post-Flood, God explicitly allows meat-eating, which contradicts the idea that it’s merely a fallen condition.

3. **"Sacrifices were symbolic, pointing to Christ."**

   * ✔️ Christian theology says animal sacrifices prefigure Jesus' sacrifice.
   * ❌ Symbolism doesn’t negate the real suffering and death of animals commanded by God.

---

### ✅ **Conclusion**

* **Yes, God allows and even commands the killing of sentient animals.**
* **No, He does not command to stop it.**
* **Yes, this raises a moral challenge** if you believe that animal suffering is inherently wrong.

* The biblical view is **species-hierarchical**: humans over animals; animal death is acceptable, human death is (sometimes) not.

---

### 💥 **Extra: The Brutal Honesty**

If you're applying modern sentience-based ethics (which says "all suffering counts morally"), then God in the Bible permits and even enjoys practices that are immoral by that standard. That includes:

* Animal sacrifice
* Animal slaughter for food
* Ritual killings without mention of mercy or pain relief

If you accept **divine command theory** (*God's permission defines morality*), then it's not immoral because God allowed it. But this is again the same "**might makes right**" logic.

## 🔥 Probable Proof: The Bible Is Speciesist

Let’s define our terms rigorously:

* **Speciesism** = The unjustified favoring of one species (usually humans) over others in moral consideration, despite equal capacity for suffering.
* **Sentience-based morality** = The view that the capacity to suffer or feel pleasure is the basis for moral consideration (humans, cows, pigs, etc., all count).

---

### 🔎 **Step 1: Bible Prioritizes Humans Over Animals**

1. **Genesis 1:26** — *“Let us make man in our image… and let them have dominion over the fish… birds… livestock… and all the earth.”*

   * Humans are explicitly given **dominion** over animals.
   * No such dominion is granted to animals over humans.

2. **Genesis 9:2-3** — *“The fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast… every moving thing that lives shall be food for you.”*

   * God endorses using animals for human food.
   * Animals are to fear and submit to humans.

3. **Exodus 20:13 vs. Leviticus 1:10-13**

   * *“You shall not murder”* applies **only to humans**.
   * Animal killing is regulated (clean/unclean) but **never forbidden**.
   * Sacrificing animals is commanded and seen as pleasing to God.

---

### 🔎 **Step 2: Bible Accepts Animal Death & Suffering As Morally Permissible**

1. **Leviticus 1–7** — Animals are ritually slaughtered to atone for human sins.

   * Their suffering is not morally questioned.
2. **Numbers 31:25-47** — Virgins are spared; cattle, flocks are plundered and sacrificed.

   * Animals are treated as property and war spoils.
3. **Luke 15:23** — The "fatted calf" is killed for a celebration.

   * Jesus gives a parable that assumes animal killing is normal and good.

---

### 🔎 **Step 3: Bible Never Grants Moral Equality to Animals**

1. **Humans are called “in God’s image” (Genesis 1:27).**

   * Animals are explicitly *not* given this status.

2. **Animals are used in worship (sacrifice) and food, not humans.**

   * No human sacrifices are commanded by God (child sacrifice is condemned).
   * Animal sacrifice is directly ordered by God.

3. **Ecclesiastes 3:21** — *“Who knows if the human spirit rises upward and if the spirit of the animal goes down into the earth?”*

   * Shows even afterlife status is uncertain or inferior for animals.

---

## ✅ **Deduction**

**Premise 1:**
Sentience-based morality says beings with the capacity to suffer deserve equal moral consideration.
(Human, dog, cow, pig, chicken all suffer, so all count morally.)

**Premise 2:**
The Bible explicitly grants dominion to humans, permits animal killing, commands sacrifice, and never grants animals equal moral status.

**Conclusion:**
Therefore, the Bible operates on a **speciesist ethic**: humans over animals, regardless of animals’ sentience or suffering.

---

## 💥 Why This Clashes with Modern Moral Principles

* **Animal Rights View (Peter Singer, Tom Regan, etc.):**
  * Species is morally irrelevant; what matters is *suffering*.
  * Killing or torturing animals is wrong if we reject racism and sexism (logical extension).

* **Biblical View:**
  * Species (humanity) is morally decisive.
  * Human benefit justifies animal suffering (food, sacrifice, dominion).

Thus, **the Bible's ethics conflict with modern universalism** where *all sentient beings* deserve moral protection.

---

## 🔥 **Extra Irony:**

Even in the *messianic future*, animal sacrifice is pictured continuing:

* **Ezekiel 40-46** — Temple sacrifices are described, implying Bible God's ultimate plan still accepts animal killing.
* This destroys any claim that animal sacrifice was just a "temporary shadow"—it’s depicted as eternal.

---

###  Verdict:

* The Bible is speciesist by clear text and practice.
* If you accept sentience-based ethics, biblical ethics on animals is **morally defective**.
* If you accept divine command theory (God defines right/wrong), then speciesism becomes divinely justified — but this is circular (might makes right).

## 1️⃣ **Biblical Ethics (Speciesist Logic)**

## 2️⃣ **Modern Universal Ethics (Anti-Speciesist Logic)**

*(Derived from thinkers like Peter Singer, Tom Regan, utilitarian ethics, and human rights logic)*

**Premise 1:**
If a being can suffer or feel pleasure, it deserves equal moral consideration.
*(Sentience is the basis of moral value.)*

**Premise 2:**
Animals (e.g., cows, pigs, chickens, dogs) are sentient and can suffer.

**Premise 3:**
Causing suffering or death without necessity is morally wrong, regardless of species.

**Conclusion:**
Therefore, killing, using, or sacrificing animals for human benefit (when not necessary) is morally wrong — same as it would be wrong to do so to humans.

| Moral System                | Status of Killing Sentient Animals                           |

## 🔎 **Expanded Formal Syllogism**

### **Biblical Speciesist Syllogism**

1. All humans are in God’s image (special moral status).
2. All animals are not in God’s image (lower moral status).
3. Killing lower moral status beings is permissible if God allows.
4. Therefore, killing animals is permissible.

### **Universal Anti-Speciesist Syllogism**

1. All sentient beings can suffer (shared moral status).
2. Causing unnecessary suffering is wrong, regardless of species.
3. Killing sentient beings causes suffering and death.
4. Therefore, killing animals is morally wrong (same principle as for humans).

## ✅ **Deduction:**

* You **cannot** logically accept both systems simultaneously without contradiction.
* If you accept that *sentience* is the basis of moral worth (modern ethics), you must reject the Bible’s ethics on animals.
* If you accept the Bible’s system (divine command and species hierarchy), then you must accept speciesism and deny universal sentience-based morality.

## 🔥 **Human Pain Dilemma**

Let’s say:

* You have **a human infant** (1 year old).
* You have **an adult pig** (or dog, chimp, etc.).

Both are:

* Sentient (they feel pain and pleasure).
* Self-aware to some degree.
* Helpless victims in this scenario.

---

### 🟡 **The Moral Question:**

If you could only save *one* from being tortured,
**who should you save — and why?**

---

## 🧩 **The 2 Options**

### Option 1:

**Say: "I save the human infant because it's human."**

* 🟥 This is speciesism — you’re using species membership as the deciding factor.
* Logically identical to racism (using race) or sexism (using gender).
* So, you must admit: **"I am a speciesist."**

---

### Option 2:

**Say: "I save whoever suffers more."**

* 🟩 This is anti-speciesist logic.
* If the pig would feel more pain, you must save the pig.
* You are now consistent with the moral principle: *minimize suffering* without species bias.

---

## 🚩 **Logical Trap: There Is No Third Option**

* If you say *"but humans are smarter!"*, that also justifies racism against less educated groups and sexism (as women historically were denied education). Intelligence is not morally relevant for pain.

* If you say *"but humans have souls!"*, that’s a religious special claim — and doesn't change the *observable* fact that pain is pain.
  (And souls are not empirically verifiable.)

* If you say *"but humans have potential!"*, so do baby animals — and also brain-damaged humans, whom we still protect.

---

## ✅ **Logical Deduction (Formal Form)**

**Premise 1:** It is wrong to cause unnecessary suffering.
**Premise 2:** Both human infants and animals can suffer.
**Premise 3:** Species membership is not morally relevant to suffering.
**Conclusion:** Therefore, prioritizing saving a human over an animal *just because* it's human is speciesism (morally wrong).

---

* If you reject racism and sexism (because they are arbitrary discrimination),
* And you claim to care about suffering,
* Then you **must** reject speciesism too — or face logical inconsistency.
