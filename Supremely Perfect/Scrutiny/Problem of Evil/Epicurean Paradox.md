# Epicurean Paradox

## Intro

The Epicurean Paradox, attributed to the ancient Greek philosopher Epicurus, is one of the earliest formulations of the Problem of Evil. It questions the compatibility of an all-powerful, all-knowing, and all-good God with the existence of evil. 

## Epicurean Paradox - Logical Reasoning

Here is the Epicurean Paradox presented in a deductive logic form:

1. P1: If God is willing to prevent evil but not able, then He is not omnipotent.
2. P2: If God is able to prevent evil but not willing, then He is malevolent.
3. P3: If God is both able and willing to prevent evil, then evil should not exist.
4. P4: If God is neither able nor willing to prevent evil, then why call Him God?
5. P5: Evil exists.
6. C: Therefore, either God is not omnipotent, not omnibenevolent, or does not exist, isn't it?

## Scrutiny

* Is it not more correct that "If God is willing to prevent evil that are not necessary for the greater good but not able, then He is not omnipotent" rather than "If God is willing to prevent evil but not able, then He is not omnipotent" ? If so, then premise one is incomplete so problematic, isn't it?

* Is it not more correct that "If God is able to prevent evil that are not necessary for the greater good but not willing, then He is malevolent" rather than "If God is able to prevent evil but not willing, then He is malevolent"? If so, then premise 2 is incomplete so problematic, isn't it?

* Are you sure that if God is both able and willing to prevent evil, then evil should not exist? What if evil is logically necessary for the greater good? What if it is necessary for the development of virtues (courage, compassion, etc.)? What if it  is necessary for the greater good we cannot perceive or comprehend? If so, then premise 3 is problematic so the conlusion will not follow, isn't it?

## Conclusion

In summary, this Epicurean paradox is problematic or questionable so it shows that the conclusion is not conclusively proven.
