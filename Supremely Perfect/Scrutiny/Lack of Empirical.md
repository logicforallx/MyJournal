# Lack of Empirical Evidence

## Logical Reasoning

P1: There is no empirical evidence for God's existence.
P2: Belief in entities without empirical evidence is irrational.
C: Therefore, belief in God is irrational.

## Scrutiny 

* Empirical is not the only kind of evidence especially if we are talking about the being that exist infinitely in time and space. How can a finite being measure the un-measurable or infinite  being? We cannot observe or measure everything in the universe, but it does not mean it does not exists, right?

* Is it true that belief in entities without empirical evidence is irrational even if they have valid reason to believe on it? Does the consciosness of others does not exist? Is it not proven empirically that the consciosness of others exist as of 24.08.18, right? Does the black hole, other scientific facts, and other parts in the universe does not exist before when we dont have yet empirical evidence for it? It does not follow, right?

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic: We directly observe the sunset illusion, optical illusion, and the likes but is it true even in this world or reality we , right?

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic: The Problem of Induction -  this approach faces challenges related to the problem of induction. Just because something has happened consistently in the past (e.g., seeing cats as cats), it doesn't guarantee it will happen in the future. There's always a possibility of an unexpected event or new information that could change our understanding. Like sun the set illusion, observing that the sun rises every day does not guarantee it will rise tomorrow, and the likes.

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic - Brain in a Vat:  This thought experiment, popularized by Hilary Putnam, forces us to question the reliability of our senses.  If a brain could be kept alive and fed information that creates a perfectly realistic simulated world,  how could we ever be sure our current reality isn't just a simulation?  If this is possible, then this challenges the idea of knowledge as based solely on sensory experience, right?

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic - The Matrix Hypothesis: Similar to the Brain in a Vat, this hypothesis suggests that our experiences could be the result of a highly advanced computer simulation, as depicted in the movie "The Matrix." If this is possible or our sensory experiences are artificially generated, it questions the reliability of our perceptions and our knowledge of reality, isn't it?

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic - The Dream Hypothesis: It suggests that our waking life could be analogous to a dream, raising questions about the reliability of our observations and perceptions. If our waking experiences are akin to dreams, then our sensory perceptions might not reflect an external reality but rather an internally generated experience. Actions within a dream have consequences within that dream context. For instance, feeling fear in a dream feels real, and the body's physiological responses (like increased heart rate) are real. And how can we know that this is now not in a dream if it's possible that we can dream also in a dream?

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic - The Simulation Argument: This argument, popularized by philosopher Nick Bostrom, suggests that if it is possible to create a perfectly realistic computer simulation of a world, and if such simulations are common, it is statistically more likely that we are currently living in one of these simulations rather than in the base reality. This challenges our assumptions about the nature of our existence and the reliability of our sensory experiences. 

* Relying solely in empirical evidence itself is problematic - Evil Demon: Rene Descartes, the philosopher, proposed the idea of an evil demon, if the supreme is a supremely powerful demon, then this being can deceives us by manipulating our senses. This raises the question of whether even our most basic beliefs about the world can be trusted. Everything we believe, every sense experience, and every thought, they could all have been put in our minds by the supremely powerfull evil genius who created an illusory world so seamless, we'd have no way of detecting the illusion.  If this is possible, what is the significance of this in knowing and choosing something right?

## Conclusion

In summary, it does not follow that belief in God is irrational if there are no empirical evidence for it.
