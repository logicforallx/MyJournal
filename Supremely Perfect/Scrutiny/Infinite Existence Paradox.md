## Intro

The Infinite Existence Paradox is a concept that delves into the nature of an infinitely existing entity or state. It raises questions about how something can exist infinitely without running into logical contradictions or other issues. Here's a possible formulation of such a paradox:

## The Infinite Existence Paradox

**Premises:**

1. **Infinite Existence:** Assume there is an entity (let’s call it Entity X) that exists infinitely, without beginning or end.
2. **Temporal Continuity:** For Entity X to be said to exist infinitely, it must be capable of existing at every moment of time—past, present, and future.

**Paradox:**

- **Temporal Boundaries:** If Entity X exists infinitely in time, it must exist at all points along the temporal axis. However, if time itself is not infinite but rather finite (as some theories suggest the universe or time could be cyclical or have boundaries), how can Entity X exist infinitely within a finite temporal framework?
    * **Scrutiny:**
    - Infinitely existing outside of time implies its existence is not limited by time, there is no point in time where Entity X does not exist. It means it is always there from the past, present, and when the future comes, it is also there. This means its existence is not limited by temporal constraints, such as whether time is finite or cyclical. So even if we assume that time is finite, does it mean that the infinite being cannot exist if it is outside of time? If it can still exist, infinite existence is not truly a paradox.
    - What if time is just a tool of the mind to measure past, present, or future?  If so, how can you apply time to un-measurable or infinite? If not, infinite existence is not truly a paradox.

- **Change and Stability:** For Entity X to exist infinitely, it must either be in a constant state of flux or remain static. If it changes, how can it maintain infinite existence if change implies progression through time? If it remains static, does it not face the problem of being somehow "fixed" in a temporal framework that could imply a point of origin or a boundary?
    * **Scrutiny:**
    - Is being unchanging or having fix inherent identies imply a point of origin? It is not identical, right? Furthermore, infinite being existence does not have origin because it is infinite. But if you are talking about the origin of other things,external to infinite being, then will the infinite being imply that it has boundaries if something that has origin are those others and not the infinite being? If so, infinite existence is not truly a paradox.
    - Does change implies not existing? what if its action or effect is just changing but its inherent identity is not? If so, infinite existence is not truly a paradox.

- **Contradiction in Definition:** The concept of "infinite existence" implies a lack of boundaries or limits. However, any form of existence might be understood within a framework that implicitly contains some form of boundary or limit (e.g., the logical framework of understanding). How can something exist without any form of boundary or limit if our conceptual understanding of existence requires some form of context or framework?
    * **Scrutiny:**
    - Infinite existence in time and space does not imply lack of boundaries or limits in everything especially in the laws of logic. Infinite existence means existing outside of time, it has no beginning and end, or it is always there. However, it does not imply limitless in everything, it is not identical, right? If so, infinite existence is not truly a paradox.
