# The Divine Hiddenness

## Intro

The Divine Hiddenness Argument is a philosophical argument against the existence of an all-loving and all-powerful God based on the premise that if such a God existed, they would make their presence unmistakably known to everyone. Here’s a formal logical deduction of the argument:

## Logical Reasoning

1. **Premise 1:** If an all-loving and all-powerful God exists, then it is his will to have a personal relationship with every non-resistant human being.
2. **Premise 2:** If an all-loving and all-powerful God wills to have a personal relationship with every non-resistant human being, then God would ensure that every non-resistant human being believes in His existence.
3. **Premise 3:** There are non-resistant human beings who do not believe in the existence of God.
4. **Premise 4:** If there are non-resistant human beings who do not believe in the existence of God, then God has not ensured that every non-resistant human being believes in His existence.
5. **Premise 5:** If God has not ensured that every non-resistant human being believes in His existence, then God does not will to have a personal relationship with every non-resistant human being.
6. **Conclusion:** Therefore, God does not will to have a personal relationship with every non-resistant human being.
7. **Premise 6:** If God does not will to have a personal relationship with every non-resistant human being, then an all-loving and all-powerful God does not exist. 
8. **Final Conclusion:** Therefore, an all-loving and all-powerful God does not exist.

## Scrutiny 

* Premise 1, 2 and 5 is problematic or questionable, even if someone is all loving and wills to have a relationship with every non-resistant human being, it is possible that it is alright for them if someone will not love them back. Like there are someone who donate, feed animals, doing public service and the likes but it is ok for them to not have gain for it. If so, the whole argument is not conclusively proven in strict logical sense, right?

* Premise 1, 2 and 5 is problematic or questionable. If we are speaking of a Supremely Perfect being, a being that has all logical perfection, then it does not require anything for others, as it has all it needs, which is an example of perfection. So it does not require anything for others like for someone to love them back. If so, the whole argument is not conclusively proven in strict logical sense, isn't it?

* Premise 1, 2 and 5 is problematic or questionable. Even it is God wills to have a personal relationship with every non-resistant human being, it does not mean that it will force that to happen as it is possible that God is altruistic. If so, the whole argument is not conclusively proven in strict logical sense, isn't it?

* Human understanding is limited, and infinite ways are higher than finite ways. Is it not possible that God has reasons for remaining hidden that are not accessible to human reasoning but are part of a greater, coherent divine plan? If so, Divine hiddenness does not necessarily negate the existence of an all-loving and all-powerful God; it may just reflect the mysterious nature of divine wisdom, right?

## Conclusion

In summary, the whole argument is not conclusively proven in strict logical sense.
