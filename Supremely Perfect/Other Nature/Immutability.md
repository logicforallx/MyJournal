# Immutability (Unchanging)

## Does the Supremely Perfect Being is unchanging?

## Definition

1. Something is **Supreme**, if and only if, it is the highest, greatest or the maximal. [Definition]
2. Something is **Perfect**, if and only if, it is a property that is absolutely positive and free from faults or defects (negative). [Definition]
3. Something is **Positive**, if and only if, it is good, affirmative, or constructive quality or attribute. In other words, it is a property that is in accordance with a being. [Definition]
4. Therefore, something is **Supremely perfect being**, if and only if, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely positive and free from faults or defects (negative). In other words, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely in accordance (good) with a being and free from faults or defects (badness). [Implication] from [1,2,3]
5. Something is **Pure**, if and only if, it not mixed or adulterated with any other thing(s). [Definition]
   * **For example:**
   - **Pure water** does not contain not water.
   - **Pure white** does not contain not white.
   - **Pure goodness** does not contain not good.
   - **Pure positive** does not contain not positive.

**Note:** A definition may vary and not universally applicable, depends on the context. But in the context of this meditation, those definitions are strictly defined as that.

## Logical Reasoning 1

1. Something is **Supremely perfect being**, if and only if, it is maximal in perfection (a property that is absolutely positive and pure - free from faults or defects (negative)). [Implication] from [Definition]
2. If perfection is always perfect, then it is more perfect: *For if perfection is always perfect, then that perfection actualize at all instances, which is more perfect than a perfection that does not actualize at all instances.* [Implicaion]
3. Maximal in perfection does not lack perfection. In other words, it has all perfection: *For 'all' is the maximum of something.*  [Implication]
4. So, if a being is maximal in perfection, then it has all perfection that are always perfect (unchanging) [2,3]
5. So, If supremely perfect being exist, then does it have all perfection that are always perfect (unchanging)? [1,4]

## Logical Reasoning 2

1. Something is **Supremely perfect being**, if and only if, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely positive and pure - free from faults or defects (negative). [Implication] from [Definition]
2. If something is maximal in absolute and pure positive property, then it has all absolute and pure positive property: : *For 'all' is the maximum of something.*  [Implication]
3. If something is **Supremely perfect being**, then it has all absolute and pure positive property, or does not lack absolute and pure positive property in other words. [1,2]
4. Something is **absolute**, if and only if, it is not qualified or diminished in any way; total; complete.  [Definition]
5. Any change involves an addition (qualified) or subtraction (diminished) of qualities (property, attribute, or characteristic):
    1. Something is a **quality**, if and only if, it is a property, attribute, or characteristic of an entity (e.g., "redness," "height," "being good"). [Definition]
    2. Something **change**, if and only if, it has a difference in the qualities of an entity between two moments in time. [Definition]
    3. Every changing entity can be described by a set of qualities at any given moment:  
        - Let Qt1 = {q1,q2,...,qn} represent the qualities of entity X at time t1.
        - Let Qt2 = {q'1,q'2,...,q'm} represent the qualities of entity X at time t2.
        - This is [Implication] from [1,2]
    4. Change occurs if and only if Qt1 is not Qt2; If Qt1 is Qt2 there is no change. [Implication] from [3]
    5. If Qt1 is not Qt2, then there must be at least one quality that: 
        - Exists in Qt2 but not in Qt1 (addition) [Implication] from [3]
        - Exists in Qt1 but not in QT2 (subtraction) [Implication] from [3]
    6. Therefore, Any change (alteration) in something must involve either: 
        - Addition of a quality (e.g., gaining redness)
        - Subtraction of a quality (e.g., losing redness)
        - Or both
        - This is [Implication] of [4,5]
    *  **Examples and Clarifications**
        - **Qualitative Change**:  
        - A leaf turning from green to red:  
            - Subtraction: Loses the quality "green."  
            - Addition: Gains the quality "red."  
        - **Quantitative Change**:  
        - A tree growing taller:  
            - Subtraction: Loses the quality "height = 10m."  
            - Addition: Gains the quality "height = 12m."  
        - **Relational Change**:  
        - A book moving from a table to a shelf:  
            - Subtraction: Loses the quality "on the table."  
            - Addition: Gains the quality "on the shelf."  
6. If something is absolute property, then it cannot change. [4,5]
7. Therefore, if something is **Supremely perfect being**, then it has all unchanging and pure positive property, or does not lack unchanging and pure positive property in other words. [3,6]
    * **For example:**
    * If q is a perfection and X has all perfection, then X is always q.
    * If 'absolutely and purely good to all' is a perfection and X has all perfection, then X is always 'absolutely and purely good to all'.
    * If 'actual existence' is a perfection and X has all perfection, then X is always 'actually existing'.

* **Note:** Occurence of action, interaction and the likes is different from inherent identity (qualities, characteristic, property, etc). See justification on scrutiny.
