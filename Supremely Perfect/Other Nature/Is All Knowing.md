# Is the Supremely Perfect Being All knowing? Is it All Knowing

## Definition

1. Something is **Supreme**, if and only if, it is the highest, greatest or the maximal. [Definition]
2. Something is **Perfect**, if and only if, it is a property that is absolutely positive and free from faults or defects (negative). [Definition]
3. Something is **Positive**, if and only if, it is good, affirmative, or constructive quality or attribute. In other words, it is a property that is in accordance with a being. [Definition]
4. Therefore, something is **Supremely perfect being**, if and only if, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely positive and free from faults or defects (negative). In other words, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely in accordance (good) with a being and free from faults or defects (badness). [Implication] from [1,2,3]
5. Something is **Omniscient (All Knowing)**, if and only if, it knows everything or it is knowing everything. [Definition]
6. Someone has **knowledge** for something, if and only if, it has awareness (perceive or understand) for it. [Definition]
7. Something is **Pure**, if and only if, it not mixed or adulterated with any other thing(s). [Definition]
   * **For example:**
   - **Pure water** does not contain not water.
   - **Pure white** does not contain not white.
   - **Pure goodness** does not contain not good.
   - **Pure positive** does not contain not positive.

**Note:** A definition may vary and not universally applicable, depends on the context. But in the context of this meditation, those definitions are strictly defined as that.

## Logical Reasoning 

**Is Supremely Perfect Being all knowing? Many philosophers suggest that a Supreme is all knowing, however, it also means that it knows how to actualize evil, know meaningless knowledge, etc. Why will it waste it's time in knowing that, if it only has good/positive property? Is the Supreme truly an all-knowing being?**

1. Something is **Supremely perfect being**, if and only if, it is maximal in perfection (absolute and pure goodness).  [Implication] from [Definition]
2. Maximal in perfection does not lack perfection. In other words, it has all perfection: *For 'all' is the maximum of something.* [Implication]
3. Knowledge to actualize all perfection is necessary to have more (added) perfection: 
    1. If someone is perfect (in other sense) and has absolute intention to actualize all perfection, then it has more (added) perfection (absolute and purely good property): *For absolute intention is the example of property, as intention means concious will, which can be a property of a concious being, so absolute intention is a property. So, if someone is perfect and has absolute intention for perfection, then it is more (added) perfection.*  [Implication]
    2. Absolute intention to actualize all perfection necessitates knowledge to actualize all perfection: *For absolute intention (concious will) for something necessitates knowledge or awareness for something, as absolute conscious will for something requires *  [Implication]
    3. Knowledge to actualize all perfection is necessary to have more (added) perfection. [1,2]
4. So, if a being is maximal in perfection, then it has at least knowledge to actualize all perfection (absolute and pure goodness). [2,3]
5. So, if supremely perfect being exist, then does it have at least knowledge to actualize all perfection (absolute and pure goodness)? [1,4]
