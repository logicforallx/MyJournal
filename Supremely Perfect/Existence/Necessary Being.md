# Necessary Being

**Why is there something rather than nothing?**

The argument for the existence of a **necessary being** (often identified with God in classical metaphysics) is rooted in the Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR) and is central to Leibniz’s cosmological argument. Below is the **logical structure** of the argument as formulated by Leibniz, drawn from his works (e.g., *Monadology*, *Theodicy*) and reconstructed in syllogistic form:

## **Leibniz’s Argument from a Necessary Being**

1. **Premise 1 (Contingency of the World):**  
   *"The world (or the totality of contingent things) exists, but it is not self-explanatory. Each contingent thing has a cause or reason for its existence outside itself."*  
   (Contingent beings depend on external causes; their non-existence is logically possible.)
   - If I doubt the observation or reality for that, then that doubt begin to exist, which is an example of dependent or contingent from something, like dependent from the existence of this idea.

2. **Premise 2 (Principle of Sufficient Reason):**  
   *"There must be a sufficient reason for the existence of the entire series of contingent beings, as no infinite regress of contingent causes can provide a complete explanation."*  
   (An infinite chain of contingent causes does not resolve the question *why there is something rather than nothing*; the PSR demands an ultimate explanation.)
   - This is possibly because of the casual adequacy principle and infinite regress of dependency impossibility, documented in '\\Supreme Being\Existence\Ex nihilo nihil fit.md' and '\\Supreme Being\Existence\Infinite Regress.md'.

3. **Premise 3 (Necessity of a Sufficient Reason):**  
   *"The sufficient reason for the totality of contingent beings cannot itself be contingent, as this would reintroduce the need for further explanation."*  
   (To avoid infinite regress, the ultimate reason must be *necessary*—i.e., independent/self-explanatory and existing by its own nature.)
   - This is also documented in '\\Supreme Being\Existence\Infinite Regress.md'.

4. **Conclusion (Existence of a Necessary Being):**  
   *"Therefore, there must exist a necessary being (God) whose existence is self-grounding and provides the sufficient reason for all contingent beings."*  
   (This being is *absolutely necessary*, uncaused, and contains within itself the reason for its existence.)

## **Key Leibnizian Textual Support**  
- **From *Monadology §38*:**  
  *"There must be a sufficient reason for contingent truths, or truths of fact […] a sufficient reason [found] in a necessary being which carries the reason for its existence within itself."*  

- **From *Theodicy §7*:**  
  *"This ultimate reason for things is called God. […] In him lies the sufficient reason for all existence, which cannot be found in the aggregate of contingent beings."*  

## **Formal Syllogism**  
1. **Contingent beings exist.**  
2. **Contingent beings require a sufficient reason for their existence.**  
3. **The sufficient reason for all contingent beings cannot be another contingent being or an infinite series of such beings.**  
4. **Therefore, the sufficient reason must be a necessary being (God).**
