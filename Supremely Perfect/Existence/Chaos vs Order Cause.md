What Came First?
Order is often regarded as the starting point in many Western philosophical systems (Plato, Aristotle, Plotinus, Leibniz).
Chaos is often seen as a manifestation or consequence of the process of order coming into being, as in Heraclitus and certain Eastern philosophies like Taoism.
In Hegelian philosophy, chaos and order are intertwined, with order ultimately emerging from the dialectical resolution of chaos.
So, while many thinkers suggest order as the ultimate starting point (whether through God, reason, or the One), some traditions, particularly Heraclitus and Taoism, see chaos and order as interdependent rather than oppositional, where both play roles in the unfolding of reality.

In conclusion, while many philosophers argue for order coming first, chaos is typically seen as a necessary counterpart in the process of the world's unfolding, either as a transitional phase or as a dynamic part of the order itself.
