# Unchanged Changer

 If we're talking about **Aquinas' First Cause**, then **it must be an *unchanged changer*** (or *unchangeable changer*, or *unmoved mover*). And here’s the **logical deduction** why:

---

## 🔁 1. **Change Requires a Cause**
- To change is to go from *potential* to *actual* (e.g., cold water becoming hot: it had the potential to be hot, and then it actually becomes hot).
- But **nothing can actualize its own potential** (you can't give yourself something you don’t have).
- Therefore, whatever is changing must be changed by something else — a cause.

---

## 🔗 2. **No Infinite Regress of Changers**
- If A is changed by B, and B by C, and so on, then we’d have an infinite regress of dependent changes.
- But if every changer requires another changer, **then nothing would ever actually change**, because the whole chain would never get started.
- There must be a **first** in the series — a cause that causes change without itself needing to be changed.

---

## 🧱 3. **Therefore, the First Cause Is Unchanged**
- This *first changer* must not be changed by anything else.
- That means it has **no potential** needing actualization — it is **pure act** (actus purus in Aquinas’ terms).
- It must be **immutable** (unchangeable), otherwise it would require a cause.

---

## 🔥 Why This Makes Sense Logically

Imagine a fire heating iron:
- The iron gets hot because the fire is hot.
- The fire is the actual cause of the iron’s potential to be hot becoming actual.
- But if the fire itself needed to be heated by something else, and that by another, etc., you’d never get hot iron — unless there’s something in the chain that is hot *by nature*, not by being made hot.

👉 That *first hot thing* = *unchanged changer* = *pure actuality* = *First Cause* = *God*.

---

## ❗ Why It *Must* Be Unchanging

If it **changed**, then:
- It would be moving from potentiality to actuality.
- Therefore, it would need a cause.
- But the *First Cause* is, by definition, the first — it cannot need a prior cause.

Thus:  
✅ To be First Cause → must not change → must have no potential → must be fully actual → **unchanged changer**.

---

## 🔚 Summary

The First Cause **must be an unchanged changer** because:
1. All change involves the actualization of potential.
2. Nothing can actualize its own potential.
3. Therefore, all change is caused by something else.
4. There cannot be an infinite regress of causes.
5. So there must be a First Cause that is not itself changed.
6. This First Cause must be **unchanging**, i.e., an *unchanged changer*.
