# C.S. Lewis Moral Argument


C.S. Lewis's **moral argument** for the existence of God is a well-known reasoning that he presented in *Mere Christianity*. Below is a formalized version of the argument, based on his writings and reflections:

---

### 1. **Premises**
   1. **Premise 1 (Existence of a Moral Law):** All human beings recognize some objective standard of right and wrong, which Lewis refers to as the "Moral Law" or "Law of Nature."
   2. **Premise 2 (Moral Law is Not Subjective):** This Moral Law is not merely a product of human instinct, culture, or personal preference but transcends individual opinions.
   3. **Premise 3 (Moral Law Requires a Moral Lawgiver):** The existence of an objective moral standard implies the existence of a source or lawgiver that is independent of humanity.
   4. **Premise 4 (The Moral Lawgiver is Personal):** Only a personal being can be the source of such moral standards because laws and obligations make sense only in the context of persons.

---

### 2. **Argument Development**
   1. **Human Awareness of Moral Law:**  
      - People universally argue about right and wrong, justice and injustice, which shows they implicitly believe in a standard of morality that applies to all.
      - For example, when someone says, "That's unfair!" they appeal to a standard beyond mere personal preference.

   2. **Moral Law as More than Instinct:**  
      - Humans have multiple instincts (e.g., the instinct to protect oneself and the instinct to help others), but the Moral Law acts as a guide that tells us which instinct to follow in any given situation.
      - The Moral Law cannot be reduced to mere survival mechanisms or social constructs because it often calls us to act against self-interest or societal norms.

   3. **Moral Law as Objective:**  
      - If morality were purely subjective or cultural, we would have no basis to criticize practices like slavery, genocide, or other atrocities.
      - Objective morality requires a foundation beyond human agreement or evolution.

   4. **The Need for a Moral Lawgiver:**  
      - Laws and obligations require an origin. If the Moral Law is objective and universal, it must come from a source beyond humanity.
      - An impersonal force (like nature or physics) cannot impose moral duties; only a personal being can create and communicate obligations.

   5. **The Moral Lawgiver as God:**  
      - The Moral Lawgiver must be perfectly good, as the standard of morality requires a source that embodies moral perfection.
      - This leads to the conclusion that the source of the Moral Law is God, who is both personal and perfectly good.

---

### 3. **Conclusion**
   - **Summary:** The existence of an objective Moral Law, recognized universally by humanity, points to the existence of a transcendent, personal, and perfectly good Moral Lawgiver—namely, God.

---

### Simplified Version (in Lewis's Words)
1. If there is no God, there is no objective moral law.  
2. There is an objective moral law.  
3. Therefore, God exists.
