# Dependence of the Self

## **The Argument from the Dependence of the Self**

Descartes' argument from the dependence of the self can be formalized in the following way, based on his reasoning in the *Meditations*:

### **Formal Argument and Possible Justification

1. **I exist as a thinking being:** (Descartes’ famous "Cogito" argument).
2. **My existence is not self-sustaining:** Possibly because he observe that he does not have the power to cause or maintain his own existence at every moment (He observe that he is finite and imperfect being).
3. **If something exists and continues to exist, it must have a cause or explanation for its continued existence** This is because, the otherwise implies something came from nothing, which is illogical. This is justified in another file in "Ex nihilo nihil fit.md".
4. **I cannot be the cause of my own continued existence** Possibly because he observe that he is finite and imperfect (lack the necessary power to maintain his existence).
5. **Therefore, my existence must depend on something else that has the power to sustain it:** This is base from  1 to 4 premises.
6. **The being that sustains my existence must be independent and self-sustaining:** This is possibly because, what he mean is what ultimately sustain his existence. If it's not independent also, then it implies infinite regress of dependency, which is illogical. This is justified in another file in "Infinite Regress.md"
7. **Only a perfect being can be self-sustaining and independent:** A perfect being, by implication on this context, must have necessary existence and power or perfection to be self-sustaining and independent.
8. **Therefore, the being that sustains my existence must be God (A perfect being):** This is base from 5 to 7 premises.
9. **Therefore, God must exist to explain my continued existence:** This is base from #8.

* Problem of the "Self-Sustaining" Concept: Descartes claims that a self-sustaining being is needed to explain the existence of the self. However, the concept of "self-sustenance" might be unclear or overly simplistic. If we understand “self-sustenance” as the ability to maintain existence without external cause, how do we account for the laws of nature that govern a self's continued existence? For example, biological processes or physical laws may sustain a human’s life, not just the idea of God. Therefore, one might argue that existence doesn't require an external "perfect" being; instead, it might rely on natural processes that don't involve divine causality.
  - Even if we assume that a natural process can be cause, it must be still self sustaining and independent, which means perfect in that sense, which Descartes called God. This is because, otherwise means something came from nothing, which is illogical, right?

* Dependency Doesn't Necessarily Imply God: Descartes argues that his existence must depend on something "self-sustaining" and "perfect" (i.e., God). But could dependency be satisfied by something else that isn’t perfect or even divine? For instance, the self could depend on physical systems or universal laws that don't require an intervention of a perfect, personal being. It's also conceivable that dependence could be on an impersonal, necessary structure of the universe rather than a personal God. Dependency doesn't logically necessitate perfection, especially in the sense Descartes presupposes.
