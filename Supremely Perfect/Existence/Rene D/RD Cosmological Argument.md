# RD Cosmological Argument

## Intro 

**Meditations on First Philosophy (Meditation III)**: Descartes also uses a form of the cosmological argument, which is based on the existence of himself and the need for a cause of his existence.

## Formal Argument with the Possible Justification:

1. **I exist as a thinking being:** If he deny or doubt his existence, then that is also example of existing. So this is proven unto himself. [Implication]
2. **Everything that begins to exists must have a cause for its existence:** This is because, nothing comes from nothing. If something exist without a cause, that means something came from nothing. [Axiom]
3. **I am not the cause of my own existence because I am finite and imperfect:** This is possibly because, he observe that he is growing. If something is growing, then it has beginning, which means finite. Finite cannot cause itself because it must exist first before it can begin it's existence, which means it must exist first when it is not yet existing, which is contradiction. [Observation] and [Implication]
4. **My existence cannot be explained by an infinite regress of finite causes (e.g., parents, ancestors):** This is justified in another document, in "Infinite Regress.md". [Axiom]
5. **There must be a first cause (or explanation) that possesses the necessary power and perfection to bring about and sustain my existence.** This is base from [4] that the first cause is logically necessary for finite causes. Furthermore, it is also base on casual adequacy principle as it means it is the first cause of everything that begin to exist, then it means it must have a necessary power and perfection to bring about the existence of everything that begins to exist. This is also because, otherwise means something came from nothing, which is illogical. 
6. **Conclusion**: Therefore, does God, a perfect being, must exist as the first cause?
