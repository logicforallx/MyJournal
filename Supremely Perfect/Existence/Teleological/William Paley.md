# William Paley’s Watchmaker Argument (Natural Theology, 1802)

William Paley's  argument, as presented in *Natural Theology* (1802), centers on the analogy of a watch found on a heath to argue for an intelligent designer of nature. Below is a concise summary of his **argument** and reformulation from modern adaptations like computers or DNA (which were unknown in his time):

## Definition 

Here is the possible definition of the following words used in the meditation. Note that this definition may apply only to this meditation.

* Someone is **intelligent**, if and only if, it has the ability to apply knowledge and skills purposefully (intentionally).
* Someone is a **maker**, if and only if it has causal power or skill to assemble something.
* Someone is a **designer**, if and only if it is who plans the form, look, or workings of something before its being made or built.

## **Paley's Argument**  

**Premise 1 (Artifact Analogy):**  
If we discover a watch on a heath, its intricate design (gears, springs, purposeful arrangement) necessarily implies the existence of a watchmaker.  

**Premise 2 (Nature’s Superior Complexity):**  
Biological systems (e.g., the human eye, the structure of a bird’s wing, the mechanics of the solar system) exhibit even greater complexity, subtlety (precision), and adaptation than human-made contrivances like watches.

**Conclusion (Design Inference):**  
Therefore, the complexity and purposefulness observed in nature demand an intelligent designer, whom Paley identifies as God.  

*The possible justification for each premise is documented below in the reformulation of this argument.*  

## **Key Features of Paley’s Argument**  

1. **Focus on Mechanical Analogies:**  
   Paley uses watches, telescopes, and other 18th/19th-century mechanical devices to illustrate design. He compares these to biological structures (e.g., the eye’s lens, muscles, and nerves) and celestial mechanics (e.g., planetary orbits).  

2. **Emphasis on Purpose (“Contrivance”):**  
   He stresses that natural systems, like artifacts, show *means arranged for a purpose* (e.g., the eye is structured for seeing), which cannot arise by chance.  


## **Paley’s Own Words**  

From *Natural Theology*:  
> “Every indication of contrivance, every manifestation of design, which existed in the watch, exists in the works of nature; with the difference, on the side of nature, of being greater and more, and that in a degree which exceeds all computation.”  

He repeatedly emphasizes nature’s “contrivances” (e.g., the human eye’s lens, the fertilization of plants) as evidence of a designer, not abstract systems or information theory.
