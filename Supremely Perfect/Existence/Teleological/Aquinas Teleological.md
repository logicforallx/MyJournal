# Aquinas Teleological Argument

Here's the **structure** of Aquinas’ *Fifth Way*, the **Teleological Argument (Argument from Design)**, as found in the *Summa Theologiae* I, Q. 2, Art. 3.

---

## **The Fifth Way: Argument from Governance (Teleological Argument)**

> “The fifth way is taken from the governance of the world.” — *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 2, a. 3

## **Textual Structure (Logic Translated from Latin):**

1. **Observation of Goal-Directedness in Nature:**
   > "We see that things which lack knowledge, such as natural bodies, act for an end, and this is evident from their acting always, or nearly always, in the same way, so as to obtain the best result."

   - **Key Idea:** Non-rational natural objects (e.g., trees, stars, rivers) act in regular and purposeful ways toward ends (goals) even though they do not have intelligence.
   - Example: The acorn becomes an oak; the eye is suited for seeing.

2. **Goal-Directedness Implies Direction by Intelligent Designer:**
   > "Hence it is plain that they achieve their end, not fortuitously, but designedly."

   - Aquinas argues that consistent order cannot come from chance.
   - Natural things are directed toward an end by intention or design.
   - This is because, they don’t have knowledge of those ends. It is more possible that only something with knowledge and (intention or design or will) can direct to an end. For the otherwise or by chance would more possibly produce outcomes that are random and inconsistent. Yet we see natural objects—like plants growing, bodies functioning, and orbits of celestial bodies—consistently achieving specific ends.
   - Watch implies watch maker (intelligent designer of watch), cup implies cup maker, and so on. So the world implies world maker (Intelligent designer of world).
   - If calculator, cellphone, computer and the likes that acts towards it end or purpose requires an intelligent designer. Much more the more complex, organize and advance than that like human brain, human anatomy, this world itself, and the likes that begin to exist, right? It is more possibly requires an intelligent designer.
   - This might because also of the following:
      1. If something has no knowledge and no intent (will) for its end (goal) and it is acting toward its end, then that act toward its end is not self-determined by itself alone. [Axionm] *This is more possible, than the contrary.*
      2. If something is acting toward its end and not by itself alone, then that act is dependent on something external. [Implication]
      3. There can be no infinite regress of such dependencies. [Axiom]
      4. Therefore, if something has no knowledge and no intent (will) for its end (goal) and it is acting toward its end, then it requires something or someone with knowledge and intention that directs it toward that end. [1,2,3]
      5. If someone has knowledge and intention that directs toward the end or purpose of something, then it is intelligent or knowledgeable designer. [Implication]
      6. Therefore, if something has no knowledge and no intent (will) for its end (goal) and it is acting toward its end, then it requires an intelligent designer that directs it toward that end. [4,5]

3. **Only Intelligent Designer Can Direct to an End:**
   > "Now whatever lacks knowledge cannot move towards an end unless it be directed by some being endowed with knowledge and intelligence—as the arrow is directed by the archer."

   - Non-intelligent designer beings require something intelligent designer to direct them to their proper ends.
   - Analogy: An arrow needs an archer; it doesn’t hit the target by itself.
   - Justification is also in #2.

4. **Conclusion: Existence of an Intelligent Being:**
   > "Therefore some intelligent being exists by whom all natural things are directed to their end; and this being we call God."


## **Structure in Modern Logical Form:**

1. Non-intelligent things act regularly toward an end (goal-directed behavior is observable in nature).
2. Things that act toward an end without knowledge must be directed by something intelligent designer.
3. Therefore, there must be some intelligent designer being by whom all natural things are directed toward their end.
4. This we call God.
