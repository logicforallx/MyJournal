# Pythagoras

Below is a reconstruction of the **possible teleological argument** attributed to Pythagoras (as interpreted by Iamblichus in his *Life of Pythagoras*), framed in a syllogistic structure. This argument posits that the mathematical order of the cosmos implies an intelligent, divine principle governing it. Iamblichus’s account merges early Pythagorean ideas with Neoplatonic theology, so this is best understood as a *Neoplatonic-Pythagorean* teleological argument:

“Number is the ruler of forms and ideas, and the cause of gods and daemons.” - motleybytes.com

## Reconstructed as a deductive syllogism:

**Premise 1 (reported):** “Number is the ruler of forms and ideas, and the cause of gods and daemons.”

**Premise 2 (explicated):** If something “rules” or “causes” the structure of all things, it must itself be an intelligent principle.

**Conclusion:** Therefore the ordering of the cosmos by numerical ratios implies a rational, intelligent principle governing it.

## According to Iamblichus’s 

According to Iamblichus’s Life of Pythagoras (c. 4th century CE), that is exactly what Pythagoras taught. In Taylor’s 1818 translation:

“Number is the ruler of forms and ideas, and is the cause of Gods and dæmons.”
“He also supposes, that to the most ancient and artificially ruling deity, number is the canon, the artificial reason, the intellect also, and the most undeviating balance of the composition and generation of all things.”

In other words, Pythagoras held that number (arithmós) is the fundamental principle (ἀρχή) and ordering reason (λόγος) of the cosmos—so primary that even the gods and daimons owe their existence and structure to numerical harmony.


## **Key Elements of the Argument**  

1. **Mathematical Order as Evidence of Design:**  
   The regularities of nature (e.g., the predictable motions of stars, musical harmonies) are not accidental but reflect deliberate structure. This mirrors later teleological arguments (e.g., Aquinas’s "Fifth Way" or Paley’s "watchmaker analogy").  

2. **Divine Intellect (Nous) and Number:**  
   - The "most ancient deity" (a primal, creative divinity in Iamblichus’s hierarchy) employs number as its *τέχνη* ("art" or "craft") to impose order.  
   - Number here is both the *means* (tool) and the *expression* (logos) of divine intelligence.  

3. **Hierarchy of Being:**  
   - Even gods and daimons (intermediary spirits) are subject to numerical law, as they derive their existence from the same harmonic principles.  
   - This reflects the Neoplatonic idea of emanation: the One (divine unity) generates reality through mathematical/logical principles.  

## **Historical Context and Nuance**  
- **Pythagoras vs. Iamblichus:**  
  While Pythagoras (6th century BCE) emphasized number as the essence of reality, Iamblichus (4th century CE) reinterprets this through a Neoplatonic lens. The explicit link between number and a *self-aware divine intellect* (nous) is a later development, blending Pythagoreanism with Plato’s *Timaeus* (where a Demiurge uses geometry to craft the cosmos) and Stoic *logos* (rational world-soul).  

- **Teleology vs. Mysticism:**  
  Early Pythagoreans likely saw number as a sacred, mystical force rather than an "intelligent principle." Iamblichus’s account transforms this into a proto-theistic argument, where mathematical order becomes proof of a divine architect.  

## **Significance**  
This argument bridges **mathematics**, **theology**, and **philosophy of science**:  
- It influenced later thinkers like Kepler ("God geometrizes") and Galileo ("The Book of Nature is written in mathematics").  
- It posits that the intelligibility of the cosmos (via mathematics) reflects the rationality of its creator—a idea foundational to both classical theism and the scientific revolution.  

In essence, Iamblichus’s Pythagoras offers a **mathematical teleology**: the cosmos is a divine artifact, crafted by number, for number, and through number.
