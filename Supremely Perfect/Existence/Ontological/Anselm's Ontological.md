# Anselm’s ontological argument

## Intro

Anselm’s ontological argument is presented in his work *Proslogion* (Chapters 2 and 3). Below is the exact formulation of his argument as close to his own words as possible, in a modernized but faithful translation:

## **Anselm's Argument (Chapter 2 of *Proslogion*) with Possible Justification**

1. **Definition of God**:  
   *"God is that than which nothing greater can be conceived."*  
   (This means God is the greatest conceivable being.)
      - This is valid because definition (as long as not contradicting, honest, clear, unambiguous, and useful) for shared understanding is axiomatic as he just stated what he mean by God that he want to talk about or meditate about. This is also further justified in "\\Ways\Infinite Justification Sol\Logical\Definition & Example.md".

2. **Existence in the Mind vs. Reality**:  
   Even a fool who denies God's existence understands this concept when they hear it. Therefore, God exists at least in the understanding (in the mind).
      - While this is not justified further by Anselm, denying or doubting the existence of the idea of it, is itself an example of an existing idea (or thought, opinion, feeling) about it. So God as the greatest conceivable being exist as an idea is illogical to deny or doubt.

3. **Greater to Exist in Reality**:  
   Something that exists in both the mind and reality is greater than something that exists only in the mind.
      - This is also because, if God exists in both the mind and reality, then it has greatness in reality also and not only as an idea.

4. **Contradiction of Denying God's Existence**:  
   If God exists only in the mind, then we can conceive of a greater being—one that exists in both the mind and reality, which is a contradiction.
      - This is base from [3] and [1].

5. **Conclusion**:  
   Therefore, God must exist in reality, not just in the mind. Otherwise, God would not be "that than which nothing greater can be conceived."
      - This is base from [4] that denying God's existence is a contradiction. However, contradiction is logically impossible.


## **Anselm's Second Argument (Chapter 3 of *Proslogion*)**

Anselm extends the argument to address **necessary existence**:

1. A being that cannot be thought not to exist (a necessary being) is greater than a being that can be thought not to exist (a contingent being).
   - This is possibly because, a being that cannot be thought not to exist (a necessary being) means a being that must exist in all possible worlds or existence, unlike a contingent being that may exist only is some possible worlds. Greatness that exist in all possible worlds is greater than greatness that exist in some worlds. Thus necessary being is greater than contingent being.
   - *Is a necessary being or a being that exist in all possible world is logically valid?* A Necessary being in other words is independent being while contingent in other words is a dependent being. If only dependent being exist, then it means infinite regress of dependency, which is illogical in all possible worlds as we have justified in another document in "infinite regress.md". So there must be an independent being that exist in all possible world, whether logically hypothetical or actual world, which we call a necessary being as it is logically necessary to exist.

2. If God is "that than which nothing greater can be conceived," then God must necessarily exist.  
   (For if God could not exist, then a greater being—one that necessarily exists—could be conceived.)
   - This is also base from # [1]

3. Therefore, God exists necessarily.  
   - This is reiteration of [2]
