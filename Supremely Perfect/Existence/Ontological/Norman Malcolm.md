# Norman Malcolm's Ontological Argument


## Intro 

Norman Malcolm's **Ontological Argument**, as presented in his influential 1960 paper, **"The Existence of God and the Problem of Evil,"** is a reformulation and refinement of Anselm's original argument. It focuses on the **necessity** of God's existence and rejects the idea that God's existence could be contingent or impossible. Below is a reconstruction of his **exact argument**:

## Possible Definition of Norman

* **God** is that than which nothing greater can be conceived. Df (ie. Greatest being)
* **God** is an unlimited being. Df (ie. Infinite being)

## Argument with Possible Justification.

1. Either God exists or does not exist. *Base from logical law of non-contradiction.*

2. God can neither come into existence nor go out of existence. *This might be because of the ff:*
   1. If a greatness of a being is infinitely existing in time, then it is more greater than the otherwise (finite). [Implication]
   2. God as the greatest and infinite being cannot come into existence, as it imply finite existence in time, which is finite and less greater. [1]
   3. God as the greatest and infinite being cannot go out of existence, as it imply finite existence in time, which is finite and less greater. [1]
   4. Thus, God as the greatest and infinite being can neither come into existence nor go out of existence.  [2,3]

3. If God exists, then it cannot cease to exist. [2]

4. Therefore, if God exists, it exists necessarily. [3]

5. If God does not exist, then it cannot come into existence. [2]

6. Therefore, if God does not exist, its existence is impossible. [5]

7. Therefore, God’s existence is either necessary or impossible. [1,4,6]

8. However, God’s existence is only impossible if the concept of God is self-contradictory. *This is possibly base from the following:*
   1. If the concept of God is not self-contradictory, then it exist necessarily (not impossible to exist even in the actual world): *This is base from the previous axiom and axiom s5 of modal logic: if the concept of the greatest being is not self-contradictory, then it exist not only in some possible world but also in all possible worlds, which include the actual world, as it imply the greater than the otherwise.* [Implication]
   2. So God’s existence is only impossible if the concept of God is self-contradictory. [1]

9. The concept of God is not self-contradictory. *This is possibly base from the following:*
   1. There's no self-contradictory on the definition of God as the greatest being like the square circle, triangle without side, etc. [Observation/Perception]
   2. Greatest being imply that it has all and has only absolute positive (in accordance) property, as the otherwise is less greater. [Implication]
   3. Absolute positive property implies property that is completely free from faults or defects, so which includes free from self-contradictory, as it is a defect in laws of logic or reality and it implies not in accordance (not positive). [Implication]
   4. Therefore, the concept of God as the greatest being is not self-contradictory. [1] [2,3]

10. Therefore, God’s existence is not impossible. [8,9]

11. Therefore, from 7 and 10, God’s existence is necessary. [7,10]

## **Key Ideas in Malcolm's Argument**:

1. **Necessity of God's Existence**:
   - Malcolm stresses the idea that the existence of God is either necessary or logically impossible.
   - If God's existence were logically impossible, it would mean that the concept of God is self-contradictory, which Malcolm argues is not the case. Therefore, if God's existence is possible, it must be necessary.

2. **Rejection of Contingency**:
   - According to Malcolm, it is inconsistent to argue that God's existence is merely contingent. If God is a being whose existence is logically possible, then it must exist necessarily.

3. **Logical Possibility vs. Necessity**:
   - Malcolm introduces a **modal distinction** between logical possibility and necessity. The idea is that it is logically possible for God to exist, but if its existence is possible, it must be necessary. God cannot be a contingent being, and its non-existence cannot be logically necessary.
