# Rene Descartes Ontological Argument

##  Rene Descartes Ontological Argument From Meditations on First Philosophy (Meditation V): 

Descartes presents a version of the ontological argument, which is similar to but distinct from that of St. Anselm. He argues that the concept of God is that of a supremely perfect being, and existence is a necessary attribute of perfection.
- **Key Points**:
    - A supremely perfect being would be lacking if it did not exist, which would contradict the definition of perfection.
    - Just as the nature of a triangle includes the property of having three angles that sum to 180 degrees, the nature of a supremely perfect being includes existence.
    - Therefore, God must exist, as the concept of God includes existence as an inherent quality.

*For Descartes, God's existence is as self-evident as the truths of mathematics.*

## Version B

1. **I have an idea of supremely perfect being, i.e. a being having all perfections:** This is possibly because, if he deny or doubt the existence of that idea, then it is itself an example of an existing idea (or thought, opinion, feeling) about it. It must have all perfection, because a being that has all perfection is more and the most perfect than a being that does not. [Implication]
2. **Necessary existence is a perfection:** This is possibly because of the following:
    1. If a being has non-existence, then it has no inherent identity at all, so it has no perfection at all. [Implication]
    2. If a being exists as an idea only, then it implies having an inherent identity as an idea only, so it can have perfection as an idea only. [Implication]
    3. If a being necessarily exists, then it has inherent identity as an idea and in all possible worlds, so it can have perfection more than as an idea only. [Implication]
    4. A being that has necessary existence (exists in every possible world) is more perfect than a being that does not. [1,2,3]
    5. In other words, necessary existence is necessary for more perfection. [4]
3. **Therefore, a supremely perfect being exists:** This is base from [1] and [2]

## Key Analogies Used by Descartes

Descartes employs several analogies to illustrate the necessity of existence being tied to the concept of God:

* ***Triangle Analogy**:
- A triangle's essence includes having three angles that sum to 180 degrees. While you can conceive of a triangle without observing one, you cannot conceive of a triangle that lacks this property.
- Similarly, God’s essence includes existence. While you may conceive of God, you cannot conceive of God as lacking existence. If you conceive a God without existence, then that is not a God.
