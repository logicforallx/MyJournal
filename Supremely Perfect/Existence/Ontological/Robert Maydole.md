# **Robert Maydole's Ontological Argument**  

## Intro

For **verbatim accuracy**, consult Maydole’s *"The Ontological Argument"* (2009), where he writes:  
> *"If it is possible that a supremely excellent being exists, then it is necessary that a supremely excellent being exists [...] Therefore, a supremely excellent being exists."*  

Below is a distilled version of his ontological argument, adhering to his formal structure and terminology as presented in his 2009 paper *"The Ontological Argument"*:

## **Definitions**  
1. **Perfection (P)**: A property is a perfection iff its negation is not a perfection.  
   \( P(\phi) \leftrightarrow \neg P(\neg\phi) \).  

2. **Maximal Excellence (ME)**: A being is maximally excellent iff it has all perfections.  
   \( ME(x) \leftrightarrow \forall \phi (P(\phi) \rightarrow \phi(x)) \).  

3. **Necessary Existence (NE)**: A being necessarily exists iff it exists in all possible worlds.  
   \( NE(x) \leftrightarrow \Box \exists y \, (y = x) \).  

---

## **Premises**  
1. **Coherence of Maximal Excellence**:  
   It is possible that a maximally excellent being exists.  
   \( \Diamond \exists x \, ME(x) \).  
   This is possibly because of the following:
      1. If a property is a 'great making property', its negation is a 'lesser making property'.
      2. 'Great making property' do not entail 'lesser making property'.
      3. Maximal greatness is the greatest 'great making property'.
      4. Therefore, Maximal greatness cannot entail its negation of non-Maximal greatness.
      5. Therefore, Maximal greatness is logically coherent or possible.

2. **Necessary Existence is a Perfection**:  
   Necessary existence (\( NE \)) is a perfection.  
   \( P(NE) \).  

3. **Uniqueness of Maximal Excellence**:  
   Necessarily, there is at most one maximally excellent being.  
   \( \Box \forall x \forall y [(ME(x) \land ME(y)) \rightarrow x = y] \).  

4. **Possibility-to-Necessity (S5 Axiom)**:  
   If maximal excellence is possible, it is necessary.  
   \( \Diamond \exists x \, ME(x) \rightarrow \Box \exists x \, ME(x) \).  

---

## **Proof**  
1. **Possibility Premise**:  
   \( \Diamond \exists x \, ME(x) \).  
   *(From Premise 1.)*  

2. **Necessitation**:  
   \( \Diamond \exists x \, ME(x) \rightarrow \Box \exists x \, ME(x) \).  
   *(From Premise 4.)*  

3. **Modus Ponens (1 + 2)**:  
   \( \Box \exists x \, ME(x) \).  
   *(Therefore, a maximally excellent being necessarily exists.)*  

4. **Actual Existence**:  
   Since necessary existence entails existence in the actual world, a maximally excellent being exists.  
   \( \exists x \, ME(x) \).  

---

### **Key Features of Maydole's Argument**  
1. **Second-Order Modal Logic**: Uses quantifiers over properties to define perfections, avoiding first-order ambiguities.  
2. **Anti-Polytheism**: Premise 3 ensures uniqueness, ruling out multiple "maximally excellent" beings.  
3. **Necessity from Possibility**: Leverages the S5 axiom (\( \Diamond P \rightarrow \Box \Diamond P \)) to derive necessity.
