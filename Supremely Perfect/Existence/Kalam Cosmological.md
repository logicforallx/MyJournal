# Kalam Cosmological Argument

The Kalam Cosmological Argument is a modern formulation of the cosmological argument for the existence of God. It was most prominently defended by William Lane Craig, though its roots trace back to medieval Islamic philosophers like Al-Ghazali.

Here is the  standard form of the Kalam Cosmological Argument:

## Kalam Cosmological Argument (Formal Version)

1. Whatever begins to exist has a cause.
    - This is because, the otherwise implies something come from nothing, which is impossible, documented in '\\Supreme Being\Existence\Ex nihilo nihil fit.md'.

2. The universe began to exist.
    - As of 2025.4.8. The standard model of cosmology describes the universe as having expanded from a singularity ~ billion years ago.

3. Therefore, the universe has a cause.


Let’s analyze **what kind of cause** could bring the **entire universe** into existence.

### 🔁 1. **Uncaused**  

**Why?**  
Because of **infinite regress**. If the cause of the universe itself had a cause, then that cause would also need a cause, ad infinitum. This leads to a **vicious infinite regress** of causes, which is **metaphysically impossible**.

---

### 🧊 2. **Changeless (Immutable)**  

**Why?**  
Because **change requires time**. But **time began with the universe** (according to modern cosmology and general relativity as of 2025.4.8).  

If the cause existed **before** time, then it existed in a **timeless state**. But if it’s timeless, then it must be **changeless**, because **change** is a temporal concept (it involves a transition from one state to another).

---

### ⌛ 3. **Timeless (Beyond Time)**  

**Why?**  
Because **time is part of the universe**. Time, space, and matter began with the Big Bang (according to modern cosmology and general relativity as of 2025.4.8).  

The cause of time cannot be **in time**, otherwise it would be part of what it causes—which is a logical contradiction. The cause cannot come **after** or **during** the beginning of time.

---

### 🌌 4. **Spaceless**  

**Why?**  
Because **space** also began with the universe. There was no “outside” space “before” the Big Bang—space itself **emerged** with the Big Bang (according to modern cosmology and general relativity as of 2025.4.8).

So, the cause of space cannot be **within** space. It must **transcend** space, meaning it is **spaceless**.

---

### 💨 5. **Immaterial**  

**Why?**  
Because **material things** exist **in space** by definition and are subject to **change**, decay, and causality. But we’ve already deduced that the cause is **spaceless** and **changeless**.

Also, anything **material** would be **composite**, and composites require **parts** and a **builder**, implying it would **not** be the first cause as it is contingent.

So the cause must be **non-physical**—i.e., **immaterial**.

---

### 💥 6. **Extremely Powerful**  

**Why?**  
Because it created **all of space-time, matter, and energy**. That is an **immense effect**, requiring **immense causal power**.

---

### 🧠 7. **Personal**  

**Why?**  

A **timeless, changeless** cause cannot produce a **temporal effect** unless it has **volition**—the ability to **choose** to create.

If the cause were just a **mechanical** or **impersonal** cause (like gravity), then the effect (the universe) would be **eternally present**—it would **always** exist along with the cause. But the universe **began** at a point, so something must have **chosen** to bring it into being **at that moment**.

This means the cause has **will** and can make **decisions**.

✅ **Logical Deduction:** To explain why the universe began in time from a timeless cause → the cause must be **personal**.
