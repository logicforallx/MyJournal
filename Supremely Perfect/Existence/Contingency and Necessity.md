# Aquinas Argument from Contingency and Necessity

Here is the argument from Thomas Aquinas' **Third Way** (Necessary Being) as presented in *Summa Theologiae* I, Q. 2, Art. 3:

## **The Third Way: Argument from Contingency and Necessity**  
*Translated from the Latin (structure and logic):*

1. **Observation of Contingency:**  
   > "We find in nature things that are possible to be and not to be, since they are found to be generated, and to corrupt, and consequently, they are possible to be and not to be."

2. **Impossibility of Eternal Contingency:**  
   > "But it is impossible for these always to exist, for that which is possible not to be at some time is not. Therefore, if everything is possible not to be, then at one time there could have been nothing in existence."

3. **Absurdity of Universal Non-Existence:**  
   > "Now if this were true [that nothing existed at some time], even now there would be nothing in existence, because that which does not exist only begins to exist by something already existing. Therefore, if at one time nothing was in existence, it would have been impossible for anything to have begun to exist; and thus even now nothing would be in existence—which is absurd."
   > This is base also on the axiom that nothing comes from nothing, documented in '\\Supreme Being\Existence\Ex nihilo nihil fit.md'.

4. **Conclusion: A Necessary Being Exists:**  
   > "Therefore, not all beings are merely possible, but there must exist something the existence of which is necessary."

5. **No Infinite Regress of Necessary Causes:**  
   > "But every necessary thing either has its necessity caused by another, or not. Now it is impossible to go on to infinity in necessary things which have their necessity caused by another, as has been already proved in regard to efficient causes."

6. **Final Cause: God as the Self-Necessary Being:**  
   > "Therefore, we cannot but postulate the existence of some being having of itself its own necessity, and not receiving it from another, but rather causing in others their necessity. This all men speak of as God."

## **Key Structure of the Argument:**  

1. **Contingent beings exist** (things that can be or not be).  
2. If all beings were contingent, **nothing would exist now** (because contingent things require a prior cause).  
3. Since things **do exist now**, there must be **at least one necessary being** (a being that cannot not exist).  
4. This necessary being cannot derive its necessity from another (no infinite regress).  
5. Therefore, **God** exists as the **self-necessary being** (*ens necessarium*), the source of all necessity.
