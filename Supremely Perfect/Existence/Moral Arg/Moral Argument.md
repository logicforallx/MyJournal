# Moral Argument for the Existence of God (Supremely Perfect Being)

## Below is one widely cited formulation of the Moral Argument for the existence of God. 

**Note that while several philosophers have offered variations on this argument, the following version captures its core logical structure:**

1. If God does not exist, objective moral values and duties do not exist:
    * God here means a being that is objectively moral being and enforcer (executioner/imposer) of objective moral values and duties.
    1. If God does not exist, then there is no ultimate source and enforcer (executioner/imposer) for objective moral values and duties. [Implication]
    2. If there is no ultimate source and enforcer (executioner/imposer) for objective moral values and duties, then it cannot exist. *For something cannot ultimately come or happen from nothing.* [Implication]
    3. Therefore, if God does not exist, then objective moral values and duties cannot exist. *All we left is one's personal viewpoint, which is no more valid than everyone else viewpoint, which is subjective. Like spicy is more good than sweet, the preference is the subject or personal and not the object or universal.* [1,2]


2. Objective moral values and duties do exist (e.g., "Torturing innocents for fun is morally wrong" is universally/objectively true, regardless of human opinion. *Possibly, it is base by the laws of logic, and laws of logic are objective*).

3. Conclusion: Therefore, does God (a Supremely perfect being) exists?

## Logical Form

1. \( \neg G \rightarrow \neg M \)
2. \( M \)
3. \( \therefore G \)

## Scrutiny

* Even if its true that 'If God does not exist, objective moral values and duties do not exist', it will just imply that God as a being that is objectively moral being and executioner/imposer of  objective moral values and duties must exist for that values and duties to exist. It does not follow that it must be the Supremely perfect being that exist, does it not?
    * “Why think that the necessary moral enforcer must be a *Supremely Perfect Being*?” If God is **not morally perfect**, then: He could command “torture innocents” as good. But we would intuitively reject that as false. Therefore, we are **already measuring God** against some *higher* moral standard. That implies:
        - Either **morality is not grounded in God** (contradiction),
        - Or the grounding being must be **perfectly and necessarily good**—i.e., **Supremely Perfect**.
    - (OPEN)

If so, while "Torturing innocents for fun is morally wrong" is universally/objectively true, it does not follow that Objective moral values and duties do exist, does it not?
