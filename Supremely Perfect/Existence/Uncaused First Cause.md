# Uncaused First Cause

The **Cosmological Argument (Uncaused First Cause)** was formulated by Thomas Aquinas in his *Five Ways* (*Summa Theologiae*, Part I, Question 2, Article 3). This is a reconstruction of his argument:

## **The Cosmological Argument (Uncaused First Cause)**

**1. Everything that begins to exist or changes has a cause:**
In the world, we observe that things are caused. For example, a pot is caused by a potter, a child is caused by parents, and so on. These causes are **efficient causes**—agents that bring something into existence or change its state.

**2. Nothing can cause itself:**
Nothing can be the cause of its own existence. If something were to cause itself, it would have to exist *before* it existed, which is logically impossible. For example, a pot cannot create itself; it requires a potter.

**3. An infinite regress of causes is impossible:**
If every cause has a prior cause, there would be an infinite regress of causes. However, an infinite regress of causes is impossible because:  
   - In an infinite series, there is no **first cause** to explain the existence of the series itself.  
   - Without a first cause, there would be no subsequent causes or effects.  
   - Since we observe effects (e.g., the pot, the child), there must be a first cause to initiate the causal chain.
   - This is further justified in '\\Supreme Being\Existence\Infinite Regress.md'.
   
**4. Therefore, there must be an uncaused first cause (God)** 
There must exist an **uncaused first cause**—a being that is not caused by anything else but is the cause of all other things. This being is what we call **God** or the following:
   - **Uncaused**: It does not depend on anything else for its existence. For the contrary implies infinite regress of causes, which is impossible.
   - **Eternal**: It exists outside of time, as time itself is part of the created order. Plus, otherwise implies it begins to exist, which requires a cause and infinite regress of causes, which is impossible.
   - **Necessary**: Its existence is not contingent on anything else. For the contrary implies infinite regress of causes, which is impossible.
   - **Most Powerful**: It has the power to cause the existence of all other things or all power.

## Deductive Version 

1. For all things that begin to exist, they must have a cause.
2. For all or all things that begin to exist, they cannot cause themselves.
3. A series of causes that consists only of things that begin to exist cannot be infinitely regressive. (A causal series made up entirely of things that require a cause must have a starting point.)
4. Therefore, all things that begin to exist must ultimately be caused by a first cause that did not begin to exist (i.e., an uncaused cause).
