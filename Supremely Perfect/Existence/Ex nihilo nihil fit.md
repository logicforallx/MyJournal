# Ex nihilo nihil fit "Nothing comes from nothing"

## Is it true that nothing comes from nothing?

"Ex nihilo nihil fit" is a Latin phrase that translates to "Nothing comes from nothing." It expresses the philosophical principle that all things have a cause or origin; something cannot arise from absolute nothingness. This idea has roots in ancient Greek philosophy, particularly in the works of Parmenides and later refined by Aristotle. It's often used to argue for the necessity of a pre-existing cause or entity in discussions about the origin of the universe, creation, or causality in general.

However, is it true that nothing comes from nothing?

It is true simply by the definitions and logical structure of the concepts themselves. In the case of "nothing comes from nothing," the truth follows directly from the meaning of the words involved:**

2. It "Comes (begins) from", if and only if, it is the origin or a potential source of existence.  [Definition]

5. Therefore, something comes from nothing is logically impossible. In other words, **nothing comes from nothing** is analytical truth (true by implication of its own meaning). [3,4]

## Causal Adequacy Principle (CAP)

**The causal adequacy principle (CAP), or causal reality principle, is a philosophical claim made by René Descartes that the cause of an object must contain at least as much reality as the object itself, whether formally or eminently.**

*Is it true that it is manifest by the natural light that there must be at least as much reality in the efficient and total cause as in the effect of that cause?*

1. If an effect exceeds the cause in its reality or existence, then the excess of its reality or existence came from nothing: *This is because, it implies that the excess of its reality or existence cannot be accounted for by the cause or origin. This means that the excess of its reality originate from nothing.*  [Implication]
2. If the excess of its reality or existence came from nothing, then the excess of its reality or existence came from non existence, which is a logical contradiction. [Implication]
3. Therefore, it is logically impossible that the effect exceeds cause reality or existence. [1,2]
4. In other words, it is manifest by the natural light that there must be at least as much reality in the efficient and total cause as in the effect of that cause. [3]

**For Example:**    
- Heat cannot be produced in an object which was not previously hot, except by something of at least the same order of perfection as heat.
- A stone, for example, which previously did not exist, cannot begin to exist unless it is produced by something which contains, either formally or eminently everything to be found in the stone.
- In physics, when discussing conservation of energy or mass, the amount of energy put into a system must be at least equal to the energy that comes out. If a machine outputs a certain amount of energy, the cause (input energy) must be at least as great as the effect (output energy). This respects the idea that the cause must possess at least as much reality as its effect.  
- A 1 cup of water must be caused by a atleast 1 cup of water or has the capacity to produce at least 1 cup of water. 

**Mathematical Example**:  
- If \( x = 2 \), then \( y = x \) cannot be greater than 2
- If \( x = 5 \), any positive number derived from \( x \) must be \( \leq 5 \)
- If \( x = 3 \) and \( y = 4 \), then \( x + y = 7 \). The sum cannot be greater than the total provided by the inputs (3 and 4).
- If \( x = 0 \), then \( x \times y = 0 \) for any \( y \). The effect (product) here is limited by the reality of the input (zero), demonstrating that no matter what \( y \) is, a zero input yields a zero output. If both \( x \) and \( y \) are finite, the result of \( x \times y \) is also finite. Multiplying finite numbers cannot yield an infinite product.
- Finite Series: In a finite arithmetic series with \( n \) terms, each term \( a_n \) contributes a finite amount to the total sum. The total sum will be finite if all terms are finite. Only an infinite number of terms, or an unbounded series, can lead to an infinite result.
- If \( x \) is finite, any result from \( x \) must also be finite. So x must be infinite to get infinite from x.
