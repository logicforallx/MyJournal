## **The Fourth Way: Argument from Gradation and Perfection**  
*Translated from the Latin (logic and structure):*

1. **Observation of Gradation in Beings:**  
   > "Among beings there are some more and some less good, true, noble, and the like. But *more* and *less* are predicated of different things according as they resemble in their different ways something which is the maximum."  
   * It means: 
      - There are degrees of goodness, truth, being, etc. (some things are ‘more’ or ‘less’ good/true).
      - If there are degrees of goodness, truth, being, etc. (some things are ‘more’ or ‘less’ good/true), then there must be a maximum standard of goodness, truth, being, etc: 
         - If X is > Y and if there are no greatest, then by implicaton X cannot be greater than Y, which is a contradiction
         - Let say I have a bottle of water. This bottle has more water at time 1 than time 2. If there are no more than water at time 1, then it just imply that it is the most of it than the time 2 and others. But if there are no most of it, then that is contradiction.

2. **The Necessity of a Maximum Standard:**  
   > "Therefore, there must be something which is truest, best, noblest, and consequently *maxime ens* (most being). For those things that are greatest in truth are also greatest in being, as is said in *Metaphysics* II."  

3. **Causal Principle of Perfection:**  
   > "Now the maximum in any genus (kind) is the cause of all in that genus, as fire, which is the maximum of heat, is the cause of all hot things. Therefore, there must also be something which is to all beings the cause of their being, goodness, and every other perfection. This we call God."

## **Key Structure of the Argument:**  

1. **Gradation in Perfection Exists**:  
   - We observe degrees of goodness, truth, nobility, and being in things (e.g., a rock is less "good" than a human, which is less "good" than an angel).  

2. **Degrees Imply a Maximum Standard**:  
   - Terms like "more" or "less" only make sense relative to an **absolute maximum** (e.g., "hotter" implies a "hottest").  

3. **The Maximum is the Cause of All Degrees**:  
   - The highest perfection in any category (truth, goodness, being) must be the **source** of all lesser instances (e.g., fire causes heat in other things).  

4. **Conclusion: God as the Supreme Perfection**:  
   - The ultimate standard of perfection (the "maxime ens") which is God or Maximally Perfect being, is the ultimate cause of all being and perfection

## **Aquinas’ Key Premises (From His Works):**  
- **Metaphysical Gradation**:  
  > "All perfections exist in things according to their measure of participation in being (*esse*)."  
  - E.g., A dog is "good" insofar as it participates in the perfection of *being a dog*, but this goodness is derivative.  

- **Participation Theory**:  
  > "Whatever is in potentiality is reduced to actuality by something in actuality."  
  - Only a being that is *pure actuality* (no potentiality) can be the source of all perfections.  

- **God as *Ipsum Esse Subsistens***:  
  > "God is existence itself (*ipsum esse*), not a being that merely has existence."  
  - All limited perfections (e.g., goodness in creatures) are finite reflections of God’s infinite perfection.
