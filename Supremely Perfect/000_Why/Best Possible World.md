# The Best of All Possible World


## If Supremely Perfect Being (God) exist, then is this the best of all possible world?

The *best possible world argument* is often associated with discussions about the nature of God and the existence of evil, drawing on philosophical principles introduced by figures like Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz. Here's a possible formal presentation of this argument:

**Premises:**
1. **(P1)** If God exists, then God is omnipotent (all-powerful), omniscient (all-knowing), and omnibenevolent (all-good).
2. **(P2)** An omnipotent being has the power to create any possible world.
3. **(P3)** An omniscient being has complete knowledge of all possible worlds and their outcomes.
4. **(P4)** An omnibenevolent being would create the best possible world where goodness is maximized.
5. **(P5)** This world exists as the created reality.

**Conclusion:**
- **(C1)** Therefore, this world must be the best possible world.

## Our Stipulative Definition

1. Something is **Supreme**, if and only if, it is the highest, greatest or the maximal. [Definition]
2. Something is **Perfect**, if and only if, it is a property that is absolutely positive and free from faults or defects (negative). [Definition]
3. Something is **Positive**, if and only if, it is good, affirmative, or constructive quality or attribute. In other words, it is a property that is in accordance with a being. [Definition]
4. Therefore, something is **Supremely perfect being**, if and only if, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely positive and pure - free from faults or defects (negative). In other words, it is maximal in a property that is absolutely in accordance (good) with a being and free from faults or defects (badness). [Implication] from [1,2,3]
5. World is the **Best of All Possible World**, if and only if, it is a possible world with greatest or maximal positivity (goodness) as possible. [Definition]  
6. Something is a **Possible world**, if and only if, it represents the possible totality of all that exists (i.e., possible reality or the like).  [Definition] *(Note that we do not assume that this means the actual world yet)*
7. Something is **Possible** if and only if it is logical or does not violate the laws of logic (i.e., the law of identity, the law of non-contradiction, etc.). [Definition]  
8. Something is **Pure**, if and only if, it not mixed or adulterated with any other thing(s). [Definition]
   * **For example:**
   - **Pure water** does not contain not water.
   - **Pure white** does not contain not white.
   - **Pure goodness** does not contain not good.
   - **Pure positive** does not contain not positive.

**Note:** A definition may vary and not universally applicable, depends on the context, as Ludwig Wittgenstein have shown on his work. But for the context of this meditation, those definitions are strictly defined as that.
