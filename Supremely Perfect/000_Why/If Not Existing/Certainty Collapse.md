# Certainty Collapse

## **Descartes’ Argument from the Lack of God to the Collapse of Certainty and Meaning**

**P1.** Human reason and sense experience are fallible and have deceived us in the past.

**P2.** Therefore, we cannot be certain of any belief unless there is a guarantee that our faculties are trustworthy.

**P3.** Only a supremely perfect, non-deceptive God can guarantee the truth of our clear and distinct perceptions.

**P4.** If God does not exist, then there is no ultimate guarantor that our reason is entirely reliable or that the external world is not an illusion. -- For what if the supremely absolute evil being just put those that appears clear and distinct ideas about external world to our mind to deceive us? What if our senses is just an illusion produce by that being? *See descartes idea about this for further detail about this thought experiement.*

**C1.** Therefore, if God does not exist, then **epistemic certainty collapses** — we cannot know anything for sure about the external world or things outside the mind, including even mathematical truths.

**P5.** Meaning and truth depend on the reliability of thought, knowledge, and a rational order in reality.

**C2.** Hence, if God does not exist, **meaning itself becomes unstable**, since we lack any foundation to trust our reasoning or grasp objective reality.

## Descartes’ Own Words (paraphrased and quoted)

From *Meditation*:  

> “If I did not know that there was a God, and that he is not a deceiver, I would think that I was impelled to believe things that are false.”  

He concludes that **the very trust in human reason depends on the prior certainty of a perfect God** who created our minds to seek truth rather than falsehood.

## Scrutiny

* The **Cartesian Circle** Objection:
    - Descartes says:  
    > "I can trust clear and distinct ideas because God exists and is no deceiver."

    - But Descartes also says:  
    > "I know God exists because I perceive it clearly and distinctly."

    - Thus, the critic says **he is using trust in clear and distinct ideas to prove God's existence**, and then **using God's existence to trust clear and distinct ideas**.  
    This seems **circular reasoning**: he **assumes what he is trying to prove**.

    - **Simplified:**
    > 1. Trust clear ideas → Prove God → Trust clear ideas → etc.

    - **Possible Response:**
    - It seems he mean "I can not trust clear and distinct ideas if God does exists and is not no deceiver." but not "I can not trust ALL clear and distinct ideas if God does exists and is not no deceiver."
    - It seems that he did not say that everything is not certain if God does not exist. It is possible that what he mean is almost everything is uncertain if God does not exist, i.e.., truthness of external world or things outside the mind. This is because, he say that his existence is certain base from clear and distinct idea or indubitable idea, even if the hypothetical evil might be deceiving him, does it not?

Full certainty across reasoning, memory, senses about the external world (things outside one's mind) ➔ Requires God's guarantee. Thus: No vicious circle, but a two-step ladder: Cogito and clear/distinct idea ➔ God ➔ Universal Certainty.
