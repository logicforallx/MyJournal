# **Summum Bonum (Highest Good) Argument**

## **Summum Bonum (Highest Good) Argument** — Logical Breakdown

**P1. Absolute Happiness (beatitudo) is the enjoyment of the highest good.**  
🔵 **Why?**  
- By classical philosophy (Aristotle, Aquinas), "beatitudo" (absolute happiness) is defined as the *full possession or state of the ultimate good*.  
- Partial goods (money, fame, health) give partial happiness — but this **true happiness** must be *complete, lacking nothing*.

Thus, **highest happiness = enjoyment of the highest good** — not just any random or other partial good.

---

**P2. Only a supremely perfect being fully satisfies every possible desire and is itself unlimitedly good.**  
🔵 **Why?**  
- Human desire is *open-ended*: once we have X, we seek Y, then Z, as we can observe.  
- Infinite good is better than finite good, by implication.
- Finite goods cannot quench infinite longing. Only something *infinite*, *perfect*, and *ultimate* — can satisfy a will that seeks complete fulfillment.
- A "Supremely Perfect Being" (God) by definition is infinite goodness itself (perfect truth, beauty, love, being).

Thus, **only** such a Being could fully satisfy all desires without any remainder.

---

**P3. If no such being exists, then no finite good can exhaustively satisfy the human will.**  
🔵 **Why?**  
- Finite goods (pleasure, power, health) are always *limited*, by implication.  
- Infinite good is better than finite good, by implication.
- The human heart is restless — after one success, it seeks another, by implication.  
- If there is **no infinite Good**, then **only finite goods remain** — and finite goods can never fill an infinite or better longing, by implication.  
- Therefore, **perfect happiness becomes impossible**.

---

**C. Therefore, absolute, final happiness is impossible without God.**  
🔵 **Logical Consequence:**  
If true happiness = complete satisfaction,  
and complete satisfaction requires an Infinite Good,  
and no Infinite Good exists without God,  
**then final, perfect happiness cannot exist without God**.

## 🧠 Quick Visual Map

| Step | Logical Content | Why? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| P1 | True happiness = enjoyment of the ultimate good | Classical definition of happiness |
| P2 | Only God (Supremely Perfect Being) can be ultimate good | Human longing is infinite |
| P3 | No God → Only finite goods → Never full satisfaction | Finite cannot fulfill infinite |
| C | Therefore, no God → No final happiness | Necessity of Infinite Good |

---

## 🔥 In Short:

> **Finite things can't fulfill an infinite desire.**  
> **Only the Infinite (God) can do that.**  
> **Without God, happiness will always be partial, incomplete, restless.**

## Scrutiny


* Relationships, family, love, or art can make us completely happy.
    - **Temporary fulfillment** is not **final fulfillment**.  Relationships, family, and art are *finite*: They are subject to loss (death, betrayal, aging). They themselves are not infinite in beauty, goodness, or love. **If what makes you happy can be lost,** then your happiness is unstable — it is *contingent*, *vulnerable*. Absolute happiness requires something that **cannot be lost** and **cannot fail**. Thus, **only an infinite, perfect being** (God) could provide *indestructible*, *complete* fulfillment.

    ---

* Maybe endless progress or striving (not perfection) is happiness. 
    - Endless striving in that sense presupposes that the goal is *never achieved*. **Unending desire** is, by definition, **unfulfilled desire** — *it is a sign of imperfection*. Absolute happiness, however, is **complete satisfaction**, not perpetual dissatisfaction. Imagine forever chasing water you never drink — that's restlessness, not beatitudo (perfect happiness). Therefore, **endless progress** cannot satisfy the heart’s desire for *final* rest.
    - And even if that is the case and If the Supremely/Infinite perfect being exist, that Endless progress or striving must not imply lacking satisfaction or the perfection is not their yet. It may just imply that the finite entity realizing, experiencing, observing, etc the infinite perfection overtime or continuously while they are satisfied as they already know that perfection is already there and possibly they just have move or find the way to see/experience it, to continuously experiencing it, so possibly they will just move/progress peacefully and satisfied, and satisfaction is fully realizing overtime and continuously infinitely, does it not? If so and if continues experiencing of satisfaction infinitely is better than the otherwise, then it is more good, does it not? On the other hand, it is not the same as the finite good, which means that even when enjoyed continuously, it remains limited: one can imagine a “maximal finite good,” after which there is nothing more to receive, does it not?
    - Infinite Good:
        - Truly unbounded, so every “moment” of enjoyment can be richer than the last—yet you are never “done,” because there is always more to experience.
        - Since the Good itself is inexhaustible, every stage of “progress” is genuinely new and satisfying, with no endpoint.
    - To have continuous, non-deficit satisfaction, one must have an inexhaustible source of goodness at each moment.
    - Only an infinite Good can supply new, never-repeating goodness ad infinitum.
    - Therefore, “continuous satisfaction” as described still presupposes an infinite Good—i.e. the Supremely Perfect Being.
    ---

* Maybe there is no 'infinite' desire — we're just biologically conditioned to want things.
    - Even if this is the case, infinite goodness is better than finite goodness, by implication.
    - Biology explains the *mechanism* of desire, but not its *depth* or *infinity*. Humans are uniquely capable of wanting **universal, infinite things**: Infinite truth (we never want ignorance), Infinite love (we seek infinite love), Infinite beauty (we long for lasting beauty).Probably, animals *seems* don’t seek infinite truth or unconditional meaning — **only humans do**. Thus, the *nature* of human desire points beyond biology — it demands a Good that is not finite.

    ---

* What if death itself limits our desires? Maybe we don't need infinite fulfillment because we die.
    - Is it not possible for Supremely perfect being to make someone to live again infinitely to experience infinite goodness?
