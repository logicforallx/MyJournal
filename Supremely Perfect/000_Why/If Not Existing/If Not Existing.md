# If Supreme (God) Does Not Exist

## If Supremely Perfect Being (God) does not exist, what is the implication of it?

### **Teleological (Purpose) Argument**  

**P1.** All purposeful systems require an overarching/highest telos (end) to unify subordinate ends.  
**P2.** If there is no divine (Supremely perfect or absolute) telos, there is no ultimate end to which all other ends point.  
**P3.** Without an ultimate end, human projects have no final significance.  
**C.** Thus life lacks objective purpose in a god‐less cosmos.

### **Argument from Transcendent Meaning**  

**P1.** Meaningful life requires that our actions connect to a reality greater than ourselves.  
**P2.** If God does not exist, there is no reality “greater” in a normative or standard sense—only blind matter and chance.  
**C.** Consequently, any “meaning” is merely self‐created and ultimately fragile.

###  **Argument from the Absurd** 

**P1.** Life is absurd if our search for meaning confronts an indifferent universe.  
**P2.** An indifferent universe (with no God) provides no final answers to “Why live?”  
**C.** Hence, without God, human existence is fundamentally absurd and purposeless.

### **Argument from Reason and Rationality** 

**Epiphenomenon** refers to a secondary phenomenon or effect that occurs alongside or in parallel to a primary phenomenon. 

**P1.** Rational laws (e.g. logic, mathematics) presuppose a rational source.  
**P2.** If there is no mind beyond the physical, then reason itself is an epiphenomenon of brain chemistry.  
**P3.** An epiphenomenal reason cannot justify trust in its own reliability or in objective truth, for it has no objective ultimate ground.
**C.** Thus, without God, even our capacity for truth‐seeking and understanding collapses, undermining any firm foundation for meaning.

### **Argument from Utter Contingency**  

**P1.** If everything is ultimately contingent (i.e., could have failed to exist), then nothing possesses necessary significance.  
**P2.** Without a necessary being (God), all reality is contingent.  
**C.** Therefore, there is no necessary foundation for life’s significance—meaning dissolves into chance.

### **Argument from the Lowest Common Denominator of Values**  

**P1.** If no transcendent standard exists, then all moral/value judgments reduce to personal or cultural preference.  
**P2.** Preference‐based values have no authority to criticize themselves—they are circular.  
**C.** So without God, values cannot claim real binding force; life’s “importance” is purely subjective.

### **Argument from Objective Morality**

- **Premise 1**: If God does not exist, objective moral values and duties do not exist (as they require a transcendent source).  
- **Premise 2**: Objective meaning/purpose is a subset of objective value.  
- **Conclusion**: Therefore, without God, objective meaning/purpose cannot exist.  
- **Rationale**: Purpose is tied to moral "oughts," which are argued to require a divine lawgiver.

### **Existential Necessity Argument**

- **Premise 1**: Humans intrinsically seek transcendent meaning (e.g., immortality, cosmic significance).  
- **Premise 2**: Finite, contingent beings cannot self-generate transcendent meaning.  
- **Conclusion**: Therefore, a necessary, infinite being (God) is required to satisfy this need.  

### **Argument from Ultimate Justice**

- **Premise 1**: Absolute happiness requires resolution of all injustice and suffering.  
- **Premise 2**: Naturalistic worlds cannot guarantee ultimate justice (e.g., some evils go unpunished).  
- **Conclusion**: Only a divine judge (God) ensures justice, making absolute happiness possible.  

### **Hedonic Futility Argument**

- **Premise 1**: Finite, temporary pleasures cannot satisfy the human desire for *absolute* happiness.  
- **Premise 2**: An eternal, immutable source of joy (God) is required for infinite fulfillment.  
- **Conclusion**: Without God, happiness is transient and incomplete.  

### **Meaning-Creation Paradox**

- **Premise 1**: Subjective meaning (self-created) is inherently unstable and non-binding.  
- **Premise 2**: Absolute meaning must be objective and universal.  
- **Conclusion**: Therefore, subjective meaning cannot replace divine purpose.  

### **Eschatological Argument**

- **Premise 1**: Ultimate meaning requires an eternal, unchanging endpoint (e.g., salvation, union with God).  
- **Premise 2**: Naturalism offers no eternal telos (end goal) for humanity.  
- **Conclusion**: Only God provides an eschatological purpose.  

### **Argument from Existential Longing (C.S. Lewis)**

- **Premise 1**: Humans universally desire absolute happiness/meaning.  
- **Premise 2**: Natural desires correspond to real fulfillments (e.g., hunger → food).  
- **Conclusion**: Therefore, the desire for transcendence implies God exists to fulfill it.  

### **Problem of Suffering (Inverted)**

- **Premise 1**: Suffering negates happiness unless redeemed.  
- **Premise 2**: Without God, suffering is irredeemable (no cosmic compensation).  
- **Conclusion**: Absolute happiness requires divine redemption of suffering.

### **Argument from Reason**

- **Premise 1**: Rationality (including judgments about meaning) requires reliable cognitive faculties.  
- **Premise 2**: Naturalism cannot guarantee non-arbitrary rationality (e.g., evolutionary luck).  
- **Conclusion**: Trust in reason (and thus meaning) presupposes a divine guarantor (God).  

### **Epistemological Argument**

- **Premise 1**: Knowledge of purpose requires an intentional source of information.  
- **Premise 2**: Only God (an omniscient mind) can reveal universal purpose.  
- **Conclusion**: Without God, purpose is unknowable.  

### **Ontological Argument for Meaning**

- **Premise 1**: Absolute happiness is a perfection (greatest conceivable good).  
- **Premise 2**: A perfection cannot exist contingently.  
- **Conclusion**: Therefore, absolute happiness necessarily exists in God.  

### **Argument from Personal Significance**

- **Premise 1**: Human life appears cosmically insignificant in a godless universe (e.g., "pale blue dot").  
- **Premise 2**: Significance requires being valued by a supreme consciousness.  
- **Conclusion**: God’s attention confers ultimate importance on human life.  

### **Argument from Final Satisfying Good**  

**P1.** Every finite good eventually becomes inadequate (love fades, possessions lose novelty).  
**P2.** Only an infinite Good never diminishes in its capacity to satisfy.  
**P3.** If God (the infinite Good) does not exist, no satisfaction can be ultimate.  
**C.** Thus, pure, lasting happiness is metaphysically impossible.
