# Perfect Reason

## Does everything happens for a perfect reason?

**If everything happens for a perfect reason is more Supremely perfect than not, then does a Supremely Perfect being cause or allow everything that happens for a perfect reason?**

## Stipulative Definition

* Perfect or Perfection: The condition, state, or quality of being free from all flaws or defects, as logically possible or necessary within a given context.
* Perfect Reason is the same as the reason, purpose, judgment, and the likes that is maximal and as free as logically possible or necessary from all flaws or defects.

## Logical Reasoning

1. Causing or allowing everything to happen for a perfect reason is a more perfectly right or justifiable behavior and qualities than not doing so. [Implication]
   * *This is based on its meaning or implication. The former represents a form of complete, whole, or absolutely right behavior, while the latter is null, limited, or incomplete.* 
   * *Everything or the whole is greater than its parts.*
2. Perfectly righteous implies perfectly right or justifiable behavior and qualities. [Implication]
3. Causing or allowing everything to happen for a perfect reason is a more perfectly righteous than not doing so. [1,2]
4. If a Supremely Perfect being exists, perfectly powerful and is perfectly righteous, then is everything happens for a perfect reason? [3]

*However, this doesn't necessarily mean we can always perceive or understand the perfect reason behind every event.*
