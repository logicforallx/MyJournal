# G's Existence & Nature By Anselm

St. Anselm’s reasoning about the **nature of God** stems from his ontological argument and theological reflections. His conclusions are based on **logical deduction**, starting with a fundamental definition of God as "that than which nothing greater can be conceived." Let me explain step by step why he comes to these conclusions:

---

### 1. **Starting Point: Definition of God**
   - Anselm begins with a **conceptual definition** of God:  
     > "God is that than which nothing greater can be conceived."
   - This definition serves as the foundation of his argument. For Anselm, God is the being that embodies ultimate perfection in every conceivable way.

---

### 2. **Logical Deduction from the Definition**
   - **Necessity of Perfection:**  
     - A being that is less than perfect in any way (e.g., limited in power, knowledge, or goodness) is not the greatest conceivable being.
     - Therefore, God must be perfect in all attributes: omnipotent, omniscient, omnibenevolent, and so on.
   - **Existence as a Necessary Attribute:**  
     - A being that exists is greater than one that exists only in the mind.  
     - If God exists only as a concept, then a greater being (one that exists in reality) can be conceived, which contradicts the definition of God as the greatest conceivable being.  
     - Thus, God must necessarily exist.

---

### 3. **Why God is Omnipotent (All-Powerful)**
   - **Argument:** A being that lacks power is limited and not the greatest conceivable being.  
   - **Deduction:** God must have unlimited power, meaning God is omnipotent.  
   - **Clarification:** This omnipotence is consistent with logic. God cannot perform contradictions (e.g., making a square circle), as such things are not "things" but logical impossibilities.

---

### 4. **Why God is Omniscient (All-Knowing)**
   - **Argument:** A being that lacks knowledge is less than perfect and could be surpassed by a more knowledgeable being.  
   - **Deduction:** God must have perfect and unlimited knowledge of all things, past, present, and future.  
   - **Support:** God’s existence outside of time allows for perfect knowledge of all events simultaneously.

---

### 5. **Why God is Omnibenevolent (All-Good)**
   - **Argument:** A being that lacks perfect goodness is morally inferior and not the greatest conceivable being.  
   - **Deduction:** God must be perfectly good and the ultimate source of all goodness.  
   - **Support:** Moral goodness in creation reflects the moral perfection of its source. God, as the ultimate being, must embody perfect goodness.

---

### 6. **Why God is Eternal and Immutable**
   - **Argument:** A being subject to change or bound by time is imperfect. Change implies the possibility of improvement or deterioration, which is incompatible with ultimate perfection.  
   - **Deduction:** God must exist outside time (eternal) and be unchanging (immutable).  
   - **Support:** Time and change are features of contingent, created beings. God, as the necessary being, transcends these limitations.

---

### 7. **Why God is Necessary**
   - **Argument:** A being that might not exist is contingent and dependent on something else. A contingent being cannot be the greatest conceivable being.  
   - **Deduction:** God must exist necessarily, meaning God’s existence does not depend on anything else.  
   - **Support:** If God’s existence were contingent, a greater being—one that exists necessarily—could be conceived.

---

### 8. **God as the Source of All Things**
   - **Argument:** Everything that exists must have an explanation for its existence.  
   - **Deduction:** God, as the necessary being, is the ultimate explanation for why there is something rather than nothing.  
   - **Support:** The existence of contingent beings points to a necessary being as their cause and sustainer.

---

### Anselm’s Methodology: Faith Seeking Understanding
   - Anselm’s approach is deeply rooted in faith and reason:
     - He begins with faith in the existence of God and uses reason to explore the implications of this belief.
     - He writes, "I do not seek to understand that I may believe, but I believe in order to understand."
   - His logical deductions are attempts to explain and deepen his faith-based understanding of God.

---

### Why He Concludes This About God
1. **Logical Necessity:** Starting from the definition of God as the greatest conceivable being, perfection in all attributes (existence, power, knowledge, goodness) logically follows.  
2. **Philosophical Rigorousness:** Anselm sought to demonstrate that the very concept of God necessarily implies God’s existence and nature.  
3. **Faith and Reason in Harmony:** For Anselm, the logical deductions affirm the God of Christian faith, showing that belief in God is not irrational but deeply reasonable.
