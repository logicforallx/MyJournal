Ah, got it! You're asking for the **truth tables of the five fundamental logical connectives**:

1. **Negation** (¬P)  
2. **Conjunction** (P ∧ Q)  
3. **Disjunction** (P ∨ Q)  
4. **Implication** (P → Q)  
5. **Biconditional** (P ↔ Q)  

Here are the full truth tables:

---

### 1. **Negation (¬P)**

| P | ¬P |
|---|----|
| T | F  |
| F | T  |

---

### 2. **Conjunction (P ∧ Q)**  
(True only if both are true)

| P | Q | P ∧ Q |
|---|---|-------|
| T | T | T     |
| T | F | F     |
| F | T | F     |
| F | F | F     |

---

### 3. **Disjunction (P ∨ Q)**  
(True if at least one is true)

| P | Q | P ∨ Q |
|---|---|-------|
| T | T | T     |
| T | F | T     |
| F | T | T     |
| F | F | F     |

---

### 4. **Implication (P → Q)**  
(False only if P is true and Q is false)

| P | Q | P → Q |
|---|---|-------|
| T | T | T     |
| T | F | F     |
| F | T | T     |
| F | F | T     |

#### Why this is the case:
Implication means **"if P, then Q"**. If P is false, the implication is vacuously true (since it never promised anything). It's only **false when P is true and Q fails**.

---

### 5. **Biconditional (P ↔ Q)**  
(True only if P and Q have the same truth value)

| P | Q | P ↔ Q |
|---|---|-------|
| T | T | T     |
| T | F | F     |
| F | T | F     |
| F | F | T     |
