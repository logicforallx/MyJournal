#  Major Theories and Definitions of Truth

 Here's a **comprehensive list of major theories and definitions of truth** , including traditional, modern, and postmodern perspectives — each grounded in **how truth is defined or determined by them**:

---

## 🔍 **1. Correspondence Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: A statement is **true** if it corresponds to **reality** or **facts**.

* **Example**: “The cat is on the mat” is true **if and only if** a cat is actually on the mat.
* **Strength**: Most intuitive; aligns with scientific realism.
* **Used in**: Common sense, science, philosophy of language.

---

## 🔗 **2. Coherence Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: A belief or statement is true if it **coheres** or is logically consistent with a system of other beliefs.

* **Example**: In mathematics, a theorem is considered true if it fits within the larger mathematical system.
* **Used in**: Rationalism, idealism, some forms of epistemology.

---

## ⚙️ **3. Pragmatic Theory of Truth**


> **Definition**: A statement is true if it **works**, **has practical consequences**, or is useful when applied.

* **Example**: “Vaccines prevent disease” is considered true because it yields measurable, practical results.
* **Used in**: Science, engineering, American pragmatism (Peirce, James, Dewey).

---

## 🧱 **4. Constructivist Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: Truth is constructed by **social processes**, **cultural norms**, or **linguistic frameworks**, rather than discovered.

* **Example**: The idea of “gender roles” is shaped by cultural agreement, not necessarily biological truth.
* **Used in**: Sociology, postmodernism, education theory.

---

## 🤝 **5. Consensus Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: Truth is what **everyone agrees** on within a given community under ideal conditions of discussion.

* **Example**: In democracy or law, something becomes “true” or accepted once there is consensus.
* **Used in**: Jurisprudence, Habermasian discourse ethics.

---

## 🧠 **6. Deflationary / Minimalist Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: Asserting a statement is true is nothing more than asserting the statement itself.

* **Example**: Saying “‘Snow is white’ is true” is no different than saying “Snow is white.”
* **Used in**: Analytic philosophy (e.g., Quine, Ramsey); focuses on **language use**, not metaphysics.

---

## 📉 **7. Redundancy Theory**

> **Definition**: The term “truth” is redundant; to say “It’s true that X” adds nothing to just saying “X”.

* **Example**: "It is true that the sky is blue" = "The sky is blue."
* **Used in**: Simplified linguistic philosophy; similar to deflationary view.

---

## 🪞 **8. Semantic Theory of Truth (Tarski's Theory)**

> **Definition**: Truth is a property of sentences in formal languages; a sentence is true if it corresponds to the world (the totality of entities, the whole of reality, or everything that exists).

* **Example**: “‘Snow is white’ is true **iff** snow is white.”
* **Used in**: Logic, formal semantics, computer science.

---

## 🌀 **9. Performative Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: Saying something is “true” is a **performative act** — it commits the speaker to the claim.

* **Example**: Saying “That’s true” can serve as an affirmation or social stance.
* **Used in**: Speech act theory (Austin, Searle), pragmatics.

---

## 🧬 **10. Phenomenological Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: Truth is the **unconcealing** or **disclosure** of being — when something shows itself in reality or as it really is.

* **Example**: Seeing someone’s grief not just as behavior but as the real expression of their being.
* **Used in**: Heideggerian phenomenology.

---

## 🧩 **11. Pluralist Theory of Truth**

> **Definition**: There are **multiple kinds of truth**, each valid in its own domain (e.g., moral truth, scientific truth, aesthetic truth).

* **Example**: A painting may be “true to life” in art, while a formula may be “true” in physics — both are valid truths.
* **Used in**: Contemporary philosophy, value pluralism.

---

## 🛑 **12. Nihilistic / Relativist View**

> **Definition**: Truth doesn’t objectively exist, or it is entirely **relative** to individual or cultural perspective.

* **Example**: “What’s true for you isn’t true for me.”
* **Used in**: Postmodernism, extreme subjectivism (though philosophically unstable).

---
