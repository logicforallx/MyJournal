### Foundationalism

## Problem Identification

**If proven knowledge requires justification, then how can we address Infinite regress of justification?** 

## Logical Reasoning

* Foundationalism is a philosophical theory about the structure of knowledge and justification, particularly within epistemology (the study of knowledge). It proposes that knowledge and justified beliefs are built upon a foundation of basic beliefs or axioms that are self-justifying or self-evident. These foundational beliefs serve as the starting points for reasoning and justifying other beliefs.

1. Foundational Knowledge is the same as the basic beliefs or propositions that are considered the foundation upon which other knowledge is built. They are considered to be true without requiring further justification. It is the same as Axiomatic knowledge  wich is self-evident truths or starting points (that are ultimately base in self-evident truths) in a logical system from which other propositions can be deduced.  *Note that some Foundational cannot be proven within the system or within logical reasoning except for self-evident truths.* [Implication]
2. Therefore, Foundational knowledge addresses the infinite regress of justification by providing basic beliefs that do not require further justification, thereby stopping the endless chain of reasoning. [1]

* *However, does the Foundational Knowledge exists?* Here are some foundational knowledge examples:
    * The Logical Law of Identity, states that something must be the same as itself at the same instance in the same sense, context, or meaning. This is self-evidently true or not needing to be further demonstrated or justified as true, as justification is in itself or its meaning; it is obviously true or right. Symbolically, it is expressed as "A is A".
    * The Logical Law of Non-Contradiction: This principle states that contradictory statements cannot both be true in the same sense at the same time. This is because, it will violate the logical law of identity which is impossible as we can see above. So it is considered foundational as it can be a starting points or axiom (that are ultimately base in self-evident truths) in a logical system from which other propositions can be deduced. For example, the statement "A is B" and "A is not B" cannot both be true simultaneously.
    * Basic Arithmetic Truths: Fundamental arithmetic truths, such as 1+1=2, are considered self-evident. These truths form the basis of mathematics, which is essential for various fields, including science, engineering, and economics. This is because, it will violate the logical law of identity which is impossible as we can see above, 1+1 is the same as true. So it is considered foundational as it can be a starting points or axiom (that are ultimately base in self-evident truths) in a logical system from which other propositions can be deduced.
    * Existence of the Self (Cogito Ergo Sum): Descartes' famous assertion "I think, therefore I am" suggests that the act of doubting one's own existence serves as proof of the reality of one's own mind. This foundational belief asserts that the existence of the self is undeniable and is a starting point for further philosophical inquiry.
