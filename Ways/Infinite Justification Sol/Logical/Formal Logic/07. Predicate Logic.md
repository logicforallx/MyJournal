Next, let’s dive into **Predicate Logic**, which extends propositional logic by dealing with predicates and quantifiers. Predicate logic allows us to express more complex statements involving variables and predicates that apply to those variables.

### **7. Predicate Logic**

Predicate logic, also known as first-order logic, introduces the use of predicates and quantifiers to express more detailed statements about objects within a domain.

#### **1. Predicates**

A **predicate** is a function that returns a true or false value depending on the input. It can be thought of as a property or a relation.

- **Form**: \( P(x) \) (where \( P \) is the predicate and \( x \) is the variable or object)

**Example**:
- **Predicate**: \( \text{IsEven}(x) \)
  - This predicate is true if \( x \) is an even number, and false otherwise.
- **Statement**: \( \text{IsEven}(4) \) (true, because 4 is even)

#### **2. Quantifiers with Predicates**

Combining predicates with quantifiers allows us to make statements about groups of objects.

- **Universal Quantifier**: \( \forall x \, P(x) \) means "For all \( x \), \( P(x) \) is true."
- **Existential Quantifier**: \( \exists x \, P(x) \) means "There exists an \( x \) such that \( P(x) \) is true."

**Examples**:
- **Statement**: **"All natural numbers are positive."**
  - Formal Logic: \( \forall x \, (\text{NaturalNumber}(x) \to \text{Positive}(x)) \)

- **Statement**: **"There is a natural number that is greater than 10."**
  - Formal Logic: \( \exists x \, (\text{NaturalNumber}(x) \land x > 10) \)

#### **3. Predicates with Multiple Variables**

Predicates can also have more than one variable, allowing us to express relations between objects.

- **Form**: \( R(x, y) \) (where \( R \) is the relation and \( x \) and \( y \) are variables)

**Example**:
- **Predicate**: \( \text{Loves}(x, y) \)
  - This predicate might represent that \( x \) loves \( y \).
- **Statement**: **"Every person loves someone."**
  - Formal Logic: \( \forall x \, (\text{Person}(x) \to \exists y \, \text{Loves}(x, y)) \)

#### **4. Translating Statements into Predicate Logic**

**Example Statements**:
1. **"All students in the class have taken at least one course."**
   - Formal Logic: \( \forall x \, (\text{Student}(x) \to \exists y \, \text{Course}(y) \land \text{Took}(x, y)) \)

2. **"Some people are not happy."**
   - Formal Logic: \( \exists x \, (\text{Person}(x) \land \neg \text{Happy}(x)) \)

3. **"If someone is a teacher, then they have taught a course."**
   - Formal Logic: \( \forall x \, (\text{Teacher}(x) \to \exists y \, \text{Course}(y) \land \text{Taught}(x, y)) \)
