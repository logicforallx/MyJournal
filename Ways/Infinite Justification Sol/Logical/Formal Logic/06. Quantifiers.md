Next, let's dive into **Quantifiers**. Quantifiers are essential in predicate logic as they allow us to express statements about "all" or "some" elements within a domain. Here's an overview of quantifiers:

### **6. Quantifiers**

Quantifiers are symbols used in logic to express statements involving variables. The two main types of quantifiers are the **universal quantifier** and the **existential quantifier**.

#### **1. Universal Quantifier (∀)**

The **universal quantifier** is used to state that a property or condition holds for all elements in a domain.

**Symbol**: \( \forall \) (for all)

**Form**: \( \forall x \, P(x) \) (For all \( x \), \( P(x) \) is true)

**Example**:
- Statement: **"All humans are mortal."**
- Formal Logic: \( \forall x \, (\text{Human}(x) \to \text{Mortal}(x)) \)
  - Here, \( x \) represents any human, and the statement asserts that if \( x \) is a human, then \( x \) is mortal.

#### **2. Existential Quantifier (∃)**

The **existential quantifier** is used to state that there exists at least one element in the domain for which a property or condition holds.

**Symbol**: \( \exists \) (there exists)

**Form**: \( \exists x \, P(x) \) (There exists an \( x \) such that \( P(x) \) is true)

**Example**:
- Statement: **"There is a student who passed the exam."**
- Formal Logic: \( \exists x \, (\text{Student}(x) \land \text{Passed}(x)) \)
  - Here, \( x \) represents some student, and the statement asserts that there is at least one student who passed the exam.

#### **Combining Quantifiers**

You can also combine quantifiers to express more complex statements. For example:

- **Statement**: **"Every student has a textbook."**
- Formal Logic: \( \forall x \, (\text{Student}(x) \to \exists y \, (\text{Textbook}(y) \land \text{Has}(x, y))) \)
  - Here, for every student \( x \), there exists a textbook \( y \) such that \( x \) has \( y \).

- **Statement**: **"There exists a student who knows every subject."**
- Formal Logic: \( \exists x \, (\text{Student}(x) \land \forall y \, (\text{Subject}(y) \to \text{Knows}(x, y))) \)
  - Here, there exists a student \( x \) who knows every subject \( y \).
