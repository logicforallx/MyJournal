Next, let’s move on to **Proofs** in formal logic. Proofs are essential for establishing the validity of arguments and ensuring that conclusions follow logically from premises. There are various methods of proving statements, each with its own approach and structure.

### **8. Proofs**

Proofs are structured arguments that demonstrate the truth of a statement based on given premises and rules of inference. Here are some common types of proofs:

#### **1. Direct Proof**

A **direct proof** involves deriving the conclusion directly from the premises using logical steps.

**Example**:
- **Statement**: **"If it is raining, then the ground is wet."**
- **Proof**:
  - Premise 1: \( P \to Q \) (If \( P \), then \( Q \))
  - Premise 2: \( P \) (It is raining)
  - Conclusion: \( Q \) (Therefore, the ground is wet)

  **Proof Steps**:
  1. Assume \( P \) (It is raining).
  2. From Premise 1 (\( P \to Q \)) and Premise 2 (\( P \)), apply Modus Ponens.
  3. Conclude \( Q \) (The ground is wet).

#### **2. Indirect Proof (Proof by Contradiction)**

In an **indirect proof** (or proof by contradiction), you assume the negation of what you want to prove and show that this leads to a contradiction, thereby proving the original statement.

**Example**:
- **Statement**: **"There is no smallest positive rational number."**
- **Proof**:
  - Assume the opposite: **"There is a smallest positive rational number."**
  - Let this number be \( r \).
  - Consider \( \frac{r}{2} \), which is also a positive rational number and smaller than \( r \).
  - This contradicts the assumption that \( r \) is the smallest positive rational number.
  - Therefore, the assumption is false, and the original statement is true.

#### **3. Proof by Contrapositive**

**Proof by contrapositive** involves proving the contrapositive of the statement, which is logically equivalent to the original statement.

**Example**:
- **Statement**: **"If a number is even, then its square is even."**
- **Proof**:
  - Contrapositive Statement: **"If a number's square is not even, then the number is not even."**
  - Proof:
    1. Assume the square of a number \( x \) is not even (i.e., \( x^2 \) is odd).
    2. This implies \( x \) must be odd because the square of an odd number is odd.
    3. Therefore, \( x \) is not even.

#### **4. Proof by Construction**

**Proof by construction** involves explicitly constructing an example or a model that satisfies the conditions of the statement.

**Example**:
- **Statement**: **"There exists a prime number greater than 10."**
- **Proof**:
  - Construct an example: **"11 is a prime number and greater than 10."**
  - Therefore, there exists a prime number greater than 10.

#### **5. Proof by Exhaustion**

**Proof by exhaustion** involves checking all possible cases to ensure that the statement holds true in each case.

**Example**:
- **Statement**: **"The sum of two even numbers is even."**
- **Proof**:
  - Consider all possible even numbers \( a \) and \( b \).
  - Let \( a = 2k \) and \( b = 2m \), where \( k \) and \( m \) are integers.
  - The sum is \( a + b = 2k + 2m = 2(k + m) \), which is clearly even.
  - Thus, the statement is true for all cases.

### **Practice Problems**

1. **Direct Proof**:
   - Prove that if \( x \) is an integer and \( x \) is divisible by 4, then \( x \) is also divisible by 2.

2. **Proof by Contradiction**:
   - Prove that there are infinitely many prime numbers.
   - Start by assuming there are finitely many prime numbers and derive a contradiction.

3. **Proof by Contrapositive**:
   - Prove that if a number \( n \) is not divisible by 3, then \( n^2 \) is not divisible by 9.

4. **Proof by Construction**:
   - Prove that there exists a perfect square number that is also a multiple of 12.
   - Construct an example and verify it.

5. **Proof by Exhaustion**:
   - Prove that for any integer \( n \), \( n^2 \) is either 0 or 1 modulo 4.
   - Check all possible cases for \( n \mod 4 \).
