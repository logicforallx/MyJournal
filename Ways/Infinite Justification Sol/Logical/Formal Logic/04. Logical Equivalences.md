### **4. Logical Equivalences**

Logical equivalence is when two statements have the same truth value in all possible scenarios. This means that one statement can be transformed into another without changing its truth value. There are several important laws and equivalences used to simplify and manipulate logical expressions.

#### **Common Logical Equivalences**

1. **Identity Laws**
   - **\( P \) ∧ True** = \( P \)
   - **\( P \) ∨ False** = \( P \)
   - **Explanation**: Conjunction with True or disjunction with False doesn’t change the value of \( P \).

2. **Domination Laws**
   - **\( P \) ∧ False** = False
   - **\( P \) ∨ True** = True
   - **Explanation**: Conjunction with False or disjunction with True results in the dominating value (False or True, respectively).

3. **Double Negation Law**
   - **¬(¬\( P \))** = \( P \)
   - **Explanation**: Negating a negation returns the original value.

4. **De Morgan’s Laws**
   - **¬(\( P \) ∧ \( Q \))** = (¬\( P \)) ∨ (¬\( Q \))
   - **¬(\( P \) ∨ \( Q \))** = (¬\( P \)) ∧ (¬\( Q \))
   - **Explanation**: Negation distributes over conjunction and disjunction, swapping ∧ with ∨ and vice versa.

5. **Distributive Laws**
   - **\( P \) ∧ (\( Q \) ∨ \( R \))** = (\( P \) ∧ \( Q \)) ∨ (\( P \) ∧ \( R \))
   - **\( P \) ∨ (\( Q \) ∧ \( R \))** = (\( P \) ∨ \( Q \)) ∧ (\( P \) ∨ \( R \))
   - **Explanation**: Distributes ∧ over ∨ and vice versa.

6. **Absorption Laws**
   - **\( P \) ∨ (\( P \) ∧ \( Q \))** = \( P \)
   - **\( P \) ∧ (\( P \) ∨ \( Q \))** = \( P \)
   - **Explanation**: One part absorbs the other in disjunction or conjunction.

7. **Commutative Laws**
   - **\( P \) ∧ \( Q \)** = \( Q \) ∧ \( P \)
   - **\( P \) ∨ \( Q \)** = \( Q \) ∨ \( P \)
   - **Explanation**: The order of propositions does not affect the outcome of ∧ or ∨.

8. **Associative Laws** <--- next
   - **(\( P \) ∧ (\( Q \) ∧ \( R \)))** = ((\( P \) ∧ \( Q \)) ∧ \( R \))
   - **(\( P \) ∨ (\( Q \) ∨ \( R \)))** = ((\( P \) ∨ \( Q \)) ∨ \( R \))
   - **Explanation**: Grouping of propositions in ∧ or ∨ does not affect the outcome.

9. **Implication Equivalence**
   - **\( P \to Q \)** = **¬\( P \) ∨ \( Q \)**
   - **Explanation**: An implication can be expressed as a disjunction where the antecedent is negated.

### **Examples of Applying Logical Equivalences**

1. **Simplifying Expressions**
   - Simplify **¬(P ∧ Q)**
     - Using De Morgan’s Law: **¬(P ∧ Q)** = **¬P ∨ ¬Q**

2. **Proving Equivalences**
   - Prove **P ∨ (P ∧ Q)** = **P**
     - By Absorption Law: **P ∨ (P ∧ Q)** = **P**

3. **Transforming Implications**
   - Transform **P → (Q ∧ R)**
     - Using Implication Equivalence: **P → (Q ∧ R)** = **¬P ∨ (Q ∧ R)**

4. **Using Distributive Law**
   - Simplify **P ∧ (Q ∨ R)**
     - By Distributive Law: **P ∧ (Q ∨ R)** = **(P ∧ Q) ∨ (P ∧ R)**

### **Practice Problems**

1. **Simplify** the expression **¬(P ∨ ¬Q) ∧ (R ∨ Q)**.
2. **Prove** that **(P ∨ Q) ∧ (P ∨ R)** is logically equivalent to **P ∨ (Q ∧ R)**.

Let’s work through the examples and practice problems step by step.

### **Practice Problem 1: Simplify ¬(P ∨ ¬Q) ∧ (R ∨ Q)**

We’ll use **De Morgan’s Law** and **other equivalences** to simplify the expression.

#### Original Expression:
\[
\neg(P \lor \neg Q) \land (R \lor Q)
\]

#### Step-by-Step Simplification:

1. **Apply De Morgan’s Law**:
   - De Morgan’s Law says that **¬(P ∨ ¬Q)** is equivalent to **¬P ∧ Q**. This distributes the negation over the disjunction:
     \[
     \neg P \land Q \land (R \lor Q)
     \]

2. **Distribute the conjunction** over the disjunction**:
   - Now, distribute **(\( \neg P \land Q \))** over **(R ∨ Q)** using the distributive law:
     \[
     (\neg P \land Q \land R) \lor (\neg P \land Q \land Q)
     \]
     - Notice that **Q ∧ Q** simplifies to **Q**.

3. **Simplify**:
   - Now we have:
     \[
     (\neg P \land Q \land R) \lor (\neg P \land Q)
     \]

4. **Factor out the common term** (\( \neg P \land Q \)):
   - You can factor out the common term **\(\neg P \land Q\)**:
     \[
     \neg P \land Q \land (R \lor \text{True})
     \]
   - Since **R ∨ True** simplifies to **True**, this reduces to:
     \[
     \neg P \land Q
     \]

#### Final Simplified Expression:
\[
\neg P \land Q
\]

So, the simplified form of **¬(P ∨ ¬Q) ∧ (R ∨ Q)** is **¬P ∧ Q**.

### **Practice Problem 2: Prove that (P ∨ Q) ∧ (P ∨ R) is logically equivalent to P ∨ (Q ∧ R)**

We’ll use **distributive laws** and **absorption laws** to prove this.

#### Step-by-Step Proof:

1. **Expand (P ∨ Q) ∧ (P ∨ R)** using the distributive law:
   \[
   (P \lor Q) \land (P \lor R) = P \lor (Q \land R)
   \]
   - The distributive law allows us to simplify **(P ∨ Q) ∧ (P ∨ R)** into **P ∨ (Q ∧ R)**.

#### Final Equivalence:
\[
(P \lor Q) \land (P \lor R) = P \lor (Q \land R)
\]

This proves that **(P ∨ Q) ∧ (P ∨ R)** is logically equivalent to **P ∨ (Q ∧ R)**.
