# Infallibilism

## What is knowledge? does knowledge must be infallible?

## Logical Reasoning

* Infallibilism is a theory in epistemology which suggests that for a belief to count as knowledge, it must be infallible. This means that if a belief is to be considered knowledge, it must be impossible for it to be false. [Implication]

1. Knowledge on this meditation means awareness or familiarity of the facts or truths; It is and not it is not. [Implication]
2. Awareness or familiarity of the facts or truths is not false awareness or familiarity. [Implication] [Not-AIsNotA]
3. Therefore, knowledge must be impossible for it to be false awareness or familiarity, in other words is infallible. [1,2]

* *But does infallible awareness or familiarity exist?* Here are the examples:
   * **Logical law**: Like the logical law of identity, it states that something must be the same as itself at the same instance in the same sense, context, or meaning. This is self-evidently true or not needing to be further demonstrated, explained or justified as true, as justification is in itself or its meaning; it is obviously true or right. Therefore it is infallible.
   * **Cogito, Ergo Sum**:  "I think, therefore I am" (Cogito, ergo sum). Descartes' statement is considered infallible because the act of doubting one's own existence is proof of the existence of one's mind. If you doubt, you must exist to perform the doubting.
   * **Tautologies**: "All bachelors are unmarried men." This is true by definition. The concept of a bachelor inherently includes being unmarried. It's a tautology and thus infallible.

## Scrutiny

* Not all practical knowledge needs to be infallible. Many forms of knowledge, especially empirical knowledge, are fallible but still valuable and useful.
   - While practical knowledge is often fallible, infallibilism focuses on a stricter definition of knowledge where it must be impossible to be false. This does not invalidate fallible knowledge but differentiates it from the infallible kind.

* The scope of infallible knowledge is limited. Most knowledge, particularly empirical knowledge, is not infallible and is subject to revision based on new evidence.
   - Infallibilism recognizes that while most knowledge is fallible, certain types of knowledge, such as logical laws, tautologies, and self-evident truths, are infallible. This highlights the distinction rather than diminishing the value of fallible knowledge.

* Infallibilism sets an unattainably high bar for knowledge, which may not be practical for everyday epistemic practices.
   - Infallibilism provides a theoretical framework for understanding knowledge in its most certain form. It coexists with fallibilism, which addresses practical knowledge and acknowledges its inherent fallibility.

## Conclusion

In summary, knowledge must be impossible for it to be false awareness or familiarity, in other words is infallible.
