# Socratic method

The Socratic method is a form of philosophical inquiry and dialogue developed by the ancient Greek philosopher Socrates. It is characterized by a systematic approach of asking and answering questions to stimulate critical thinking, illuminate ideas, and uncover underlying assumptions or contradictions in beliefs.

### Key Elements of the Socratic Method:

1. **Questioning**: Socrates employed a series of probing questions to challenge and explore the beliefs, knowledge, or arguments of his interlocutors. These questions are aimed at clarifying concepts, revealing inconsistencies, and encouraging deeper reflection.

2. **Irony and Elenchus**: Socrates often pretended ignorance (irony) to draw out his interlocutor's knowledge and reasoning. Through a process known as elenchus (cross-examination), he would systematically question their assumptions and assertions, guiding them towards recognizing contradictions or gaps in their understanding.

3. **Dialectic**: The Socratic method involves a dialectical process where ideas are examined through reasoned dialogue. It encourages participants to engage in critical thinking, articulate their viewpoints clearly, and defend their positions with logical arguments.

4. **Goal of Truth and Virtue**: Socrates used the method not only to uncover intellectual truths but also to pursue ethical and moral understanding. By challenging conventional wisdom and encouraging self-examination, he aimed to foster intellectual humility, moral integrity, and a deeper understanding of virtue.

### Application and Legacy:

- **Philosophy**: The Socratic method remains a foundational technique in philosophical inquiry, emphasizing critical thinking, logical reasoning, and the pursuit of truth through dialogue.

- **Education**: It has influenced educational practices by promoting active learning, discussion-based teaching, and the development of analytical skills among students.

- **Legal and Professional Settings**: The method is also used in legal education and practice, as well as in various professional contexts where reasoned argumentation and ethical reasoning are essential.

In summary, the Socratic method is a structured approach to dialogue and inquiry that seeks to uncover truth, challenge assumptions, and promote intellectual and moral development through rigorous questioning and dialectical reasoning.

      - P2: We must follow the definition of Robert and not the other. For Robert's definition is what he mean on something that he saw, not the other person has saw it.
      - P1: So you are right, Robert really saw a shape that has a 3 side on a board.
