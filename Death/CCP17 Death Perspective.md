## Perspectives on Death: Crash Course Philosophy #17

### 1. Socrates' Dilemma Argument

Socrates argues that death is either non-existence or a transition, and neither outcome justifies fear [[01:34](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D94)].

* **Premise 1:** Death leads to either a dreamless sleep (non-existence) or a passage of the soul to another life/afterlife [[01:34](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D94)].
* **Premise 2:** Dreamless sleep is peaceful, restful, and not scary [[01:41](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D101)].
* **Premise 3:** A passage to an afterlife allows for continuous learning and socializing without physical constraints (eating, resting, or bodily upkeep) [[01:46](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D106)].
* **Deduction:** Since both exhaustive possibilities lead to a non-negative outcome, **death is rationally nothing to fear** [[01:50](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D110)].
* **Corollaries:**
* If the afterlife is disembodied, cultivating the mind (philosophizing) during life yields maximum utility after death [[02:24](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D144)].
* If your main pleasures are purely physical, an afterlife without a physical body will be disappointing [[02:24](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D144)].

**My Scrutiny:**

* According to Socrates, If your main pleasures are purely physical, an afterlife without a physical body will be disappointing. However, what if everything is physical even though after life exist, If so, even the mind is physical and will die also, and it implies dissappointing also, right?

* According to Socrates, dreamless sleep is peaceful, restful, and not scary. However, it does not provide or generate good experiences, right?

* According also to him, a passage to an afterlife allows for continuous learning and socializing without physical constraints (eating, resting, or bodily upkeep). But, is it necessary that it is the case or what if it is not the case?

* If all of that is questionable, then the conclusion and implication is questionable, does it?

### 2. Epicurus’ Materialist Deduction (The Asymmetry of Existence)

Epicurus argues against fearing non-existence from a strictly materialist viewpoint [[02:51](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D171)].

* **Premise 1:** Death is the complete cessation of sensation [[03:03](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D183)].
* **Premise 2:** Good and evil (harm or benefit) only exist through subjective experience and sensation [[03:03](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D183)].
* **Premise 3:** An event cannot harm someone unless that person exists at the time the event occurs [[04:29](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D269)].
* **Deduction:** Because you and death never exist at the same time, **death cannot be bad for you at any given moment** [[04:39](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D279)]. Fearing non-existence is irrational and detracts from current well-being [[03:40](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D220)].

**My Scrutiny:**

* Even if its the case that good and evil (harm or benefit) only exist through subjective experience and sensation, is it not the case that goodness necessitate life also? If so, what if someones wants to experience more goodness, or someone wants to experience an eternal lifetime of goodnesss, will it not appear the deduction as questionable?

### 3. Thomas Nagel’s Symmetrical Loss Argument (FOMO)

Thomas Nagel evaluates the fear of death as the loss or deprivation of future good experiences [[05:06](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D306)].

* **Premise 1:** People often fear death because of missing out on future experiences (e.g., accomplishments, discovery, technological milestones) [[05:13](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D313)].
* **Premise 2:** People do not experience distress or a deep sense of loss over events that occurred *before* they were born (prenatal non-existence) [[05:26](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D326)].
* **Deduction:** By logical symmetry, feeling loss for post-mortem non-existence while being unbothered by pre-natal non-existence is inconsistent [[05:35](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D335)].
* **Qualification on Value:**
* **Sanctity of Life View:** If life is inherently good regardless of content, any loss of life is tragic [[06:04](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D364)].
* **Quality of Life View:** If life’s value depends on experience quality, a premature death (depriving a person of expected good years) is a tragedy, whereas a death ending prolonged suffering can be rationalized as a positive relief [[05:45](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D345)].

**My Scrutiny:**

* Just because that being unbothered by pre-natal non-existence  does it truly implies inconsitent for feeling loss for post-mortem non-existence? It is different case, right? Before you where born, you were not aware of goodness unlike when we were born and have awareness of goodness and also aware of idea of eternal goodness, which is more good that the otherwise, which necessitates eternal life, right?

### 4. Zhuangzi’s Natural Cycle Deduction

Zhuangzi analyzes grief and the fear of losing loved ones [[07:03](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D423)].

* **Premise 1:** Death is an inevitable part of the natural transformation cycle of life, similar to passing from infancy to childhood or adolescence [[07:14](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D434)].
* **Premise 2:** We celebrate other life milestones rather than mourning the loss of previous life stages [[07:23](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D443)].
* **Deduction:** Treating the final stage of life differently from other transitional milestones is illogical [[07:41](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D461)].
* **Corollary on Grief:** Mourning loved ones reflects fear of being left behind rather than harm done to the deceased; attempting to hold onto someone whose natural progression is moving on is selfish [[07:56](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DmjQwedC1WzI%26t%3D476)].
